Historical EXAM-STAM Scope

Exam STAM (Short-Term Actuarial Mathematics) was a professional certification administered by the Society of Actuaries. This exam is now retired. The Society of Actuaries has established the Advanced Short-Term Actuarial Mathematics (ASTAM) Exam as the official successor. The curriculum for the retired exam focused on mathematical and statistical methods for short-term insurance coverages, including severity, frequency, and aggregate loss models.

Who EXAM-STAM Was For

This exam was intended for actuarial students and professionals seeking to demonstrate proficiency in short-term insurance mathematics. Candidates must now register for the ASTAM exam to continue their professional certification journey.

How to Reuse Your Preparation

Candidates should focus on the curriculum for the ASTAM exam, which serves as the replacement. Practice applying loss distribution fitting and credibility theory to solve problems effectively.

Historical Domain Guide

Severity Models: Historical Scope

This domain covers the mathematical modeling of the size of individual losses. It involves selecting and fitting distributions to represent the severity of claims in short-term insurance scenarios.

  • Loss distribution selection
  • Parameter estimation

Frequency Models: Historical Scope

This domain focuses on modeling the exact number of insurance claims occurring within a specific period. It involves applying frequency distributions to predict claim counts for various insurance portfolios accurately.

  • Poisson distribution applications
  • Binomial distribution models

Aggregate Loss Models: Historical Scope

This domain addresses the combination of frequency and severity models to determine the total loss for an insurance portfolio. It involves calculating the distribution of aggregate losses under standard actuarial frameworks.

  • Aggregate loss calculation
  • Loss model independence

Coverage Modifications: Historical Scope

This domain examines how insurance policy features like deductibles, limits, and coinsurance modify the loss distribution. It involves adjusting ground-up loss models to accurately reflect actual insurance payments and policy terms.

  • Deductible impact
  • Policy limit application

Risk Measures: Historical Scope

This domain covers the thorough quantification of risk using various statistical measures. It involves evaluating the potential for financial loss and the uncertainty associated with different insurance claim distributions and random variables.

  • Risk quantification
  • Loss distribution analysis

Interpolation and Smoothing: Historical Scope

This domain deals with technical methods for estimating values between discrete data points and smoothing empirical distributions. It provides vital mathematical tools for refining data representations in professional actuarial modeling and analysis.

  • Interpolation methods
  • Data smoothing

Credibility: Historical Scope

This domain is the largest, accounting for 22.5% of the exam weight. It covers the use of historical data to adjust future premium estimates through credibility theory.

  • Bühlmann credibility
  • Empirical Bayes methods

Simulation: Historical Scope

This domain involves utilizing advanced computer-based simulation methods to estimate the outcomes of complex insurance models where standard analytical solutions are difficult or impossible to obtain directly for practical actuarial work.

  • Simulation techniques
  • Model estimation

Reinsurance: Historical Scope

This domain covers the practical application of core insurance principles to various reinsurance arrangements. It involves modeling the complex transfer of risk between an insurance company and a designated reinsurance provider.

  • Reinsurance structures
  • Risk transfer modeling

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The retired Exam STAM consisted of 35 questions in total. These questions were presented in a linear format and included both multiple choice and written answer types to test the candidate's knowledge of short-term actuarial models.

What is the exam price?

The price for the retired Exam STAM was 325.00 USD. This fee was paid to the Society of Actuaries for the administration of the examination.

How long is the exam?

The duration of the retired Exam STAM was exactly 210 minutes long, giving candidates this specific timeframe to successfully complete all questions administered during the testing session.

Is a renewal required for this certification?

No, a renewal is not required for this certification. The exam is retired, and the Society of Actuaries does not require ongoing renewal for this specific credential.
