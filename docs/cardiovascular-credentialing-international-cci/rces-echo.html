What This Exam Validates

The Registered Cardiac Electrophysiology Specialist (RCES) exam is administered by Cardiovascular Credentialing International (CCI). It evaluates the knowledge and skills required for professionals working in cardiac electrophysiology. The exam covers five domains, including pre-procedural, intra-procedural, diagnostic, therapeutic, and post-procedural activities. Candidates must meet specific qualification prerequisites, such as work experience or graduation from an accredited program, to be eligible for this certification. This credential demonstrates a high level of competency in the field of cardiac electrophysiology and ensures that practitioners are prepared to handle the complexities of modern cardiac care environments.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is for allied health professionals who participate in cardiac electrophysiology, cardiac device, and device-related procedures under the direct supervision of a physician. Candidates should have relevant clinical work experience or educational training in health sciences to qualify for this certification.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Preparing procedure rooms and equipment
  • Monitoring patient vital signs
  • Operating EP recording systems
  • Analyzing ECG and EGM data
  • Assisting with device implants
  • Managing post-procedural complications

How to Prepare

Review the task list and knowledge areas provided in the official exam guide. Focus on understanding cardiac anatomy, physiology, and pharmacology. Utilize recommended textbooks and consider purchasing the online self-assessment practice examinations from CCI to evaluate your readiness. Ensure you have documented your clinical experience and educational credentials as required by the application process. Consistent study of the five domains is required for success.

Domain Study Guidance

Conducting Pre-Procedural Activities: Study Guidance

This domain covers the initial steps of preparing for a procedure, including room setup, patient identification, and gathering necessary supplies to ensure safety and efficiency during the clinical workflow for all patients, accounting for 11.0 percent of the exam.

  • Equipment and radiation safety
  • Patient validation and consent

Conducting Intra-Procedural Activities: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on activities performed during the procedure, such as monitoring the patient and operating mapping and recording systems to capture accurate data throughout the duration of the clinical intervention, representing 37.0 percent of the exam content.

  • Vital sign monitoring
  • Device interrogation
  • Radiation safety protocols

Performing Diagnostic Procedures: Study Guidance

This domain involves performing diagnostic procedures, including catheter positioning, pacing stimulation, and the analysis of electrical signals to identify cardiac conditions and determine the appropriate course of action, which comprises 24.0 percent of the total examination weight.

  • ECG and EGM measurements
  • Differential diagnostic techniques
  • Catheter positioning

Assisting with Therapeutic Procedures: Study Guidance

This domain covers assisting with therapeutic interventions, including ablation procedures and the implantation of various cardiac devices to treat arrhythmias and other heart conditions under the direct supervision of a qualified physician, covering 23.0 percent of the exam.

  • Ablation equipment operation
  • Device implant assistance
  • Procedural complication management

Conducting Post-Procedural Activities: Study Guidance

This domain addresses the final stages of care, focusing on patient assessment, hemostasis, and the transition of care after the procedure to ensure the patient remains stable and recovers safely from the intervention, representing 5.0 percent of the exam.

  • Hemostasis and wound assessment
  • Post-procedure complication management
