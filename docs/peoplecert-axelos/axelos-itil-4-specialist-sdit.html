What This Exam Validates

The ITIL 4 Specialist: Sustainability in Digital and IT certification, managed by PeopleCert (AXELOS), covers the integration of sustainability into digital and IT services. It examines how organizations can align IT practices with environmental goals, focusing on carbon efficiency, energy management, and sustainability frameworks. This exam is scheduled for retirement on 2027-12-31, and no successor has been named by the certifying body. Candidates should prepare for an evaluation of how digital infrastructure impacts the environment and how to mitigate those effects through strategic management and technical improvements within the IT lifecycle.

Who Should Take This Exam

This certification is intended for IT professionals, service managers, and practitioners involved in building, deploying, or managing digital services. It is highly suitable for those aiming to apply sustainability principles to their existing IT practices and organizational processes to improve overall environmental performance.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Applying sustainability concepts
  • Utilizing sustainability frameworks
  • Embedding sustainability in IT
  • Measuring carbon emissions
  • Implementing green software practices

How to Prepare Before Retirement

Review the official PeopleCert (AXELOS) study documentation to understand the core principles of sustainability in IT. Focus on the three domains: concepts, frameworks, and practical application. Since the exam is a 90-minute linear test with 40 questions, practice applying theory to real-world scenarios. Ensure you understand the definitions of carbon efficiency and energy efficiency as described in the provided source materials. Candidates must be prepared to answer 40 questions within the 90-minute limit, requiring a strong grasp of the material to achieve the 70 passing score.

This domain addresses the practical application of sustainability, including how to embed green practices into daily IT operations and software development to reduce the overall environmental footprint of digital service delivery.
