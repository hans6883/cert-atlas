What This Exam Validates

The Certified Public Relations Counselor (CPRC) credential is provided by the Florida Public Relations Association (FPRA). It recognizes senior-level practitioners who demonstrate advanced skills in strategic public relations counseling. The assessment process evaluates a candidate's ability to apply public relations research, planning, implementation, and evaluation methods to solve complex problems and provide professional counsel for government, corporate, and nonprofit organizations across various professional environments.

Who Should Take This Exam

This certification is designed for experienced public relations practitioners who are FPRA members in good standing and hold the APR credential. Candidates should possess advanced strategic decision-making skills and a strong background handling complex communications challenges.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Strategic public relations counseling
  • Public relations research and planning
  • Implementation and evaluation methods
  • Crisis communication management
  • Oral presentation and scenario response

How to Prepare

Candidates preparing for the CPRC exam should review the official study materials provided by the Florida Public Relations Association after application acceptance. Candidates are encouraged to consult their local chapter credentialing chair for guidance and peer support. Practice applying strategic counseling framework concepts to realistic organizational case studies, focusing on both written response clarity and persuasive verbal presentation techniques to meet the candidate performance standard of 70% on each section.

Domain Study Guidance

Essay-Based Written Exam: Study Guidance

In domain 1, candidates demonstrate their strategic analytical skills through written case study responses. Candidates apply core public relations research, planning, implementation, and evaluation principles to address real-world organizational challenges and high-stakes scenarios during the exam.

  • Research and evaluation methods
  • Written case study analysis

The Oral Exam: Study Guidance

In domain 2, candidates present strategic public relations recommendations during an oral examination panel review. Candidates must deliver persuasive counsel and answer probing questions based on realistic scenario exercises evaluating communication fluency and executive posture.

  • Persuasive verbal counseling
  • Oral presentation structure

Exam-Day Guidance

For both the written essay section and oral presentation section, candidates should arrive early and review all provided instructions carefully. Ensure you are prepared to demonstrate advanced strategic counsel, structured problem solving, and clear verbal communication to the evaluation panel to achieve the required 70% passing score.

Frequently asked questions

What is the passing score for the CPRC exam?

To earn the CPRC credential, candidates must achieve a passing score of 70% on each section of the examination, including both the written essay and oral presentation components required for certification.

How is the written portion of the CPRC exam structured?

The written section requires candidates to analyze complex public relations case studies. Candidates apply strategic problem-solving methodologies to formulate detailed counselor recommendations addressing organizational challenges and public relations crises within the 1 exam domain.

What takes place during the oral examination?

During the oral exam, which is designated as domain 2, candidates present strategic recommendations based on an assigned scenario and respond to follow-up questions from evaluators, demonstrating persuasive counseling ability and professional composure.

What domains are covered on the CPRC exam?

The CPRC exam is divided into domain 1, which evaluates essay-based written problem analysis, and domain 2, which assesses verbal counseling through an oral presentation and question session to ensure candidates meet the 70% standard.
