What This Exam Validates

The ABPM American Board of Podiatric Medicine Certification evaluates candidates across ten core domains of clinical knowledge during a testing period administered by the American Board of Podiatric Medicine (ABPM). This board certification tests multiple subjects including podiatric medicine, medical imaging, pathology, public health, internal medicine, wound care, emergency medicine, anesthesiology, podiatric surgery, and general surgery across a total of 125 questions.

Who Should Take This Exam

Practicing podiatrists seeking board certification status through the American Board of Podiatric Medicine (ABPM) should take this exam. Candidates must prepare to work through clinical scenarios and multiple-choice questions.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Podiatric medical diagnosis and clinical treatment planning
  • Medical imaging interpretation including radiography and ultrasound
  • Wound care management and adjunctive therapy application
  • Surgical risk assessment and perioperative complication management

How to Prepare

Review all ten subject areas tested by the American Board of Podiatric Medicine (ABPM). Focus on understanding the clinical scenarios and multiple-choice questions that comprise the didactic and case sections of the exam. Ensure familiarity with highlighted keywords in question stems such as most, least, only, or except. Candidates should carefully manage their pacing across the 400-minute testing window to ensure every question receives adequate attention.

Domain Study Guidance

Podiatric Medicine: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers pathomechanics, interpretation of biomechanical and gait exams, orthotics, prosthetics, footwear, pedorthics, podopediatrics, and targeted podiatric trauma alongside sports medicine principles required for daily professional practice.

  • Interpretation of a biomechanical and gait exam
  • Orthotics and Prosthetics
  • Podopediatrics

Medical Imaging: Study Guidance

This domain focuses heavily on plain radiography, advanced imaging including nuclear imaging, CT, MRI, PET, and bone densitometry, diagnostic ultrasound, and complete vascular imaging and diagnostics used in podiatric patient evaluations.

  • Plain radiography
  • Advanced imaging
  • Diagnostic ultrasound

Pathology: Study Guidance

Candidates must review standard laboratory studies such as hematology, blood chemistries, coagulation studies, urinalysis, serology, synovial fluid analysis, toxicology, blood gas analysis, microbiology, and anatomic and cellular pathology findings.

  • Laboratory studies
  • Microbiology
  • Anatomic and cellular pathology

Public health: Study Guidance

This section carefully evaluates behavioral science, professional standards regarding legal and ethical obligations, research methodologies, biostatistics, and epidemiology, alongside the fundamental principles of addiction medicine relevant to clinical practitioners in the field.

  • Behavioral Science
  • Professionalism
  • Research and biostatistics

Internal Medicine & Medical Subspecialties: Study Guidance

This broad area covers endocrine, cardiology, pulmonology, care management, gastroenterology, geriatrics, hematology, oncology, infectious disease, nephrology, neurology, rheumatology, dermatology, pain management, and physical medicine topics for clinicians during patient evaluations.

  • Endocrine
  • Cardiology & Pulmonology
  • Geriatrics

Wound Care: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on wound evaluation, core treatment principles including debridement, offloading, dressings, and advanced adjunctive therapies such as negative pressure wound therapy and hyperbaric oxygen therapy for patient healing.

  • Evaluation
  • Principles
  • Adjunctive therapies

Emergency Medicine: Study Guidance

Candidates are tested on general medical emergencies as well as lower extremity emergencies that require immediate medical intervention and precise clinical management during routine and acute patient encounters across clinical practice settings.

  • General emergencies
  • Lower extremity emergencies

Anesthesiology: Study Guidance

This primary area addresses complete perioperative evaluation and risk assessment, local anesthesia delivery, general, spinal, and regional anesthesia modalities, plus the safe administration of conscious sedation during various surgical procedures.

  • Perioperative evaluation and risk assessment
  • Local anesthesia
  • Conscious sedation

Podiatric Surgery: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers surgical indications and the precise identification, prevention, and ongoing management of postoperative and intraoperative complications associated directly with podiatric surgical procedures performed by medical practitioners.

  • Surgical indications
  • Identification and management of complications

General Surgery and Subspecialties: Study Guidance

Candidates carefully examine perioperative management, general surgery principles, vascular surgery principles, plastic surgery principles, and orthopedic surgery principles relevant to complete patient care and ongoing surgical clinical practice today.

  • Perioperative management
  • General surgery principles
  • Vascular surgery principles

Exam-Day Guidance

Manage your time carefully during the 400-minute testing window to complete all 125 questions and clinical scenarios successfully without rushing through any examination section whatsoever.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The certification exam consists of a total of 125 questions distributed across the ten subject domains tested by the American Board of Podiatric Medicine during the testing period.

What is the passing score?

Please check directly with the American Board of Podiatric Medicine for the current passing score requirements, scoring methodologies, and certification maintenance details before your scheduled examination date.

How long is the exam?

The total duration allocated for the certification examination is 400 minutes, during which candidates must complete 125 questions and clinical scenarios within the designated time limit.

What subject areas are tested?

The exam tests ten distinct domains including podiatric medicine, medical imaging, pathology, public health, internal medicine, wound care, emergency medicine, anesthesiology, podiatric surgery, and general surgery.
