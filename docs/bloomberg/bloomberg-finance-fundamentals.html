What This Exam Validates

Bloomberg Finance Fundamentals (BFF) is a self‑paced, online certificate offered by Bloomberg that teaches core finance concepts through four structured domains: Accounting and Financial Statements, Corporate Finance, Asset Classes, and Portfolio Theory. The program costs $99 and uses multiple‑choice embedded assessments—two primary question types—to gauge completion. Rather than a single proctored exam, learners earn the credential by successfully finishing each module’s assessments, reflecting a completion‑based pass model with no unified pass/fail score.

Who Should Take This Exam

Early‑career finance, banking, and investment professionals, as well as students and analysts who want a Bloomberg‑issued grounding in core financial concepts before working on the Terminal. No prior certification is required, making it suitable for newcomers and seasoned practitioners alike.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Reading and interpreting financial statements
  • Applying corporate finance decision frameworks
  • Identifying characteristics of major asset classes
  • Applying portfolio theory and diversification concepts
  • Understanding capital markets and valuation basics

How to Prepare

Work through each of the four modules in sequence, taking notes on terminology as you go. Treat every embedded assessment as a checkpoint rather than a formality, and revisit any module where questions feel unfamiliar. Build a short glossary of accounting, corporate finance, and portfolio terms to reinforce retention between sessions.

Domain Study Guidance

Accounting and Financial Statements: Study Guidance

This domain covers the structure and interpretation of accounting and financial statements. Learners work through the language of balance sheets, income statements, and cash flow statements used across the industry.

  • Balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement structure
  • Core accounting terminology and ratios
  • Linking reported figures to business activity

Corporate Finance: Study Guidance

The Corporate Finance domain explores how firms allocate capital, evaluate investment projects, and choose financing structures. Learners study capital budgeting techniques, cost of capital, and the impact of financing decisions on shareholder value, linking theory to real‑world corporate strategy.

  • Capital budgeting and investment evaluation
  • Financing and capital structure considerations
  • Risk and return trade‑offs in managerial decisions

Asset Classes: Study Guidance

The Asset Classes domain surveys the primary categories of securities traded in markets, including equities, fixed‑income, cash, alternatives, and derivatives. Learners compare each class’s risk‑return profile, typical investors, and role within diversified portfolios, building a foundation for asset allocation decisions.

  • Equities and fixed-income characteristics
  • Cash, alternative, and derivative instrument basics
  • How asset classes fit different investor needs

Portfolio Theory: Study Guidance

The Portfolio Theory domain introduces the principles of combining assets to achieve optimal risk‑adjusted returns. Learners examine diversification benefits, the efficient frontier, capital‑asset pricing model concepts, and how equilibrium pricing informs modern portfolio construction and performance evaluation.

  • Diversification and portfolio construction
  • Risk‑return trade‑offs and efficient frontiers
  • Equilibrium pricing of risky assets

Exam-Day Guidance

Because BFF is self‑paced e‑learning, there is no fixed seat time or proctored session. Plan a quiet block for each module, complete its embedded assessment without rushing, and review any flagged items before moving on.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

Each of the four self‑paced modules contains its own embedded multiple‑choice assessment, and because the number of items per module is not fixed, the total number of questions a candidate encounters can differ from one learner to another, typically ranging from a few dozen overall.

What is the passing score?

The program does not use a single numeric passing score; instead, it follows a completion‑based model where learners must successfully finish the embedded assessments in all four modules. There is no separate pass/fail threshold expressed as a percentage.

How long is the exam?

Because the Bloomberg Finance Fundamentals credential is delivered entirely as self‑paced e‑learning, there is no predetermined exam duration. Candidates can allocate as much or as little time as needed to complete each module’s assessments, progressing at their own speed.

How much does the Bloomberg Finance Fundamentals certificate cost?

The Bloomberg Finance Fundamentals certificate is priced at $99, which covers access to all four self‑paced modules, embedded assessments, and the final credential. Pricing is set by Bloomberg and may be confirmed on the official product page.

Does the BFF certificate expire or require renewal?

The BFF credential does not require periodic renewal; once you have completed the embedded assessments for each of the four domains, the certificate remains valid indefinitely under Bloomberg’s policy, with no expiration date.

What topics does the BFF certificate cover?

The certification covers four core finance domains: Accounting and Financial Statements, Corporate Finance, Asset Classes, and Portfolio Theory. Together these modules provide a Bloomberg‑issued foundation in financial statement analysis, capital budgeting, securities classification, and modern portfolio construction.
