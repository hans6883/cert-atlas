What This Exam Validates

The NRC Nuclear Power Plant Operator License exam evaluates individuals seeking authorization to manipulate plant controls at nuclear facilities in the United States. Certified by the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), the licensing process tests operational competence across 6 domains: reactor theory, thermodynamics, components, nuclear power plant systems, procedures, and administrative requirements. Candidates demonstrate technical knowledge required for safe nuclear power plant operations under federal regulatory guidelines.

Who Should Take This Exam

Candidates preparing to manipulate reactor controls or direct licensed operations at nuclear power facilities should take this examination. Applicants typically complete facility training programs, medical examinations, and control manipulation requirements under U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) guidelines.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Understanding reactor theory principles
  • Applying thermodynamics to plant operations
  • Operating nuclear power plant components
  • Navigating nuclear power plant systems
  • Executing operational and emergency procedures
  • Complying with administrative requirements

How to Prepare

Study official U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) standards, including NUREG-1021 and NUREG-1122. Focus preparation on the 6 domain areas by reviewing plant system diagrams, thermodynamic concepts, reactor core physics, and site procedures. Practice answering 75 sample questions to achieve at least an 80% passing score. Coordinate with facility training staff to complete required control manipulations and simulation testing prior to taking the licensing examination.

Domain Study Guidance

reactor theory: Study Guidance

This domain covers core physics concepts, neutron multiplication, reactivity changes, and control rod worth. Operators must understand principles governing nuclear fission, reactor startup, steady-state operation, and power changes within the reactor vessel during operational conditions.

  • Core physics principles
  • Reactivity control mechanisms
  • Fission product poisoning

thermodynamics: Study Guidance

This domain addresses fluid flow, heat transfer, steam tables, and thermodynamic cycles applicable to nuclear power plants. Operators must analyze temperature dynamics, enthalpy changes, phase transitions, and heat exchanger performance to maintain proper thermal limits.

  • Heat transfer mechanisms
  • Fluid flow fundamentals
  • Thermodynamic cycles

components: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on mechanical and electrical components used in power facilities, such as valves, pumps, motors, instrumentation, and circuit breakers. Operators must understand component construction, operational indications, control logic, and failure modes.

  • Pump and valve operation
  • Electrical circuit components
  • Sensors and instrumentation

nuclear power plant systems: Study Guidance

This domain covers integrated plant systems including primary cooling, emergency core cooling, secondary steam supply, and electrical distribution. Operators must know system interlocks, flow paths, automatic actions, and control room indications across operating states.

  • Primary coolant systems
  • Emergency response systems
  • Electrical power distribution

procedures: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on normal, abnormal, and emergency operating procedures. Operators must follow step-by-step instructions for power maneuvers, system lineups, alarm response, transient mitigation, and emergency declarations to maintain safety boundaries.

  • Normal operating procedures
  • Abnormal procedure execution
  • Emergency operating guidelines

administrative requirements: Study Guidance

This domain addresses regulatory standards, shift clearance, tagout regulations, work authorization, radiation safety controls, and reporting requirements. Operators must comply with 10 CFR Part 55 rules and facility administrative guidelines during daily operations.

  • 10 CFR Part 55 compliance
  • Conduct of operations rules
  • Radiation protection standards

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early at the designated facility testing site with valid identification. Review required administrative documentation beforehand, follow all proctor instructions, and pace yourself while answering the 75 written examination questions carefully.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The NRC Nuclear Power Plant Operator License written examination consists of 75 questions. Candidates must answer these questions covering reactor theory, thermodynamics, components, systems, procedures, and administrative requirements.

What is the passing score?

Candidates must achieve a passing score of 80% on the written portion of the licensing examination. Scoring is evaluated according to U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission standards established under 10 CFR Part 55.

How long is the exam?

Specific time limits for administering the 75-question examination are governed by U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission standards and facility schedule guidelines. Candidates should confirm exact session timing with their facility training department prior to test day.

Who certifies nuclear power plant operators?

Licenses for nuclear power plant operators in the United States are issued by the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) under federal regulations detailed in Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) Part 55.
