What This Exam Validates

The CFCS: Certified Financial Crimes Specialist certification, offered by the ACFCS, validates expertise across 13 distinct domains of financial crime. The exam covers topics ranging from money laundering and fraud detection to international standards, sanctions compliance, and asset recovery. It provides an integrated framework for professionals to understand the convergence of various financial crimes and the regulatory environments that govern them on a global scale.

Who Should Take This Exam

This certification is designed for financial crime professionals, including investigators, compliance officers, auditors, and legal experts. It is suitable for those working in banking, government, or private consulting who manage anti-money laundering, fraud prevention, or asset recovery programs.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Financial crime investigation
  • Money laundering prevention
  • Fraud detection techniques
  • Corruption enforcement
  • Sanctions compliance
  • Asset recovery strategies
  • Tax evasion analysis

How to Prepare

Review the official study manual to understand the 13 domains. Focus on the commonalities between different financial crimes and how they converge in practice. Practice applying investigative techniques to real-world scenarios, such as identifying red flags in financial documents or understanding international legal frameworks. Utilize the provided practice questions to gauge your readiness for the 135-question format and ensure you are prepared for the testing environment.

Domain Study Guidance

Financial Crime Elements and Overview: Study Guidance

This domain covers the fundamental elements of financial crime and provides an overview of the field, establishing the baseline knowledge for all specialists to understand the broader scope of criminal activities and their impact on global systems.

  • Financial crime definitions
  • Commonalities of crimes

Money Laundering: Study Guidance

This section examines money laundering methods, the three distinct stages of the laundering process, and the role of various gatekeepers in identifying and preventing illicit financial flows within the global banking system and other financial institutions.

  • Three stages of laundering
  • Money laundering indicators

Corruption Enforcement and Investigation: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on corruption enforcement, the investigation of bribery cases, and global anti-corruption movements, providing specialists with the tools to identify and report corrupt practices in both public and private sectors across international jurisdictions.

  • FCPA penalties
  • Bribery investigation

Money and Commodities Flows: Study Guidance

This section details how money and commodities flow through various financial and non-financial systems, helping professionals track the movement of assets across borders and identify suspicious patterns in complex international transaction networks and trade-based money laundering.

  • Wire transfers
  • Commodity trade flows

Tax Evasion and Enforcement: Study Guidance

This domain covers tax evasion, enforcement mechanisms, and international reporting standards, ensuring that specialists understand the legal requirements and the methods used to detect tax fraud in a globalized financial environment and complex tax reporting systems.

  • Tax fraud detection
  • Reporting obligations

Fraud Detection and Prevention: Study Guidance

This section explores fraud detection and prevention strategies across various sectors, equipping professionals with the knowledge to identify internal fraud, identity theft, and other deceptive practices that threaten organizational financial integrity and overall operational security protocols.

  • Internal fraud
  • Prevention controls

Investigations: Study Guidance

This domain addresses investigative techniques for public and private financial crime cases, including the use of open-source intelligence and professional interviewing techniques to gather evidence and build successful criminal or civil cases for legal proceedings.

  • Open-source intelligence
  • Evidence collection

Cybersecurity and Privacy: Study Guidance

This section covers cybersecurity and privacy considerations in financial crime investigations, focusing on data security protocols and the legal privacy laws that govern how information is handled during the course of an investigation into financial misconduct.

  • Data security
  • Digital evidence

Sanctions Compliance: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on sanctions compliance and the regulatory frameworks that govern international trade, teaching specialists how to monitor sanctions lists and implement effective compliance controls to avoid legal and financial penalties in global business operations.

  • Sanctions lists
  • Compliance controls

Ethics: Study Guidance

This section examines ethical considerations for financial crime specialists, emphasizing the importance of professional ethics, managing conflicts of interest, and maintaining integrity while performing sensitive investigations in the financial services industry and related regulatory environments.

  • Professional ethics
  • Integrity standards

Compliance Programs and Controls: Study Guidance

This domain covers the development and management of compliance programs and controls, utilizing an enterprise approach to risk management that helps organizations mitigate the threat of financial crime through internal policies and effective oversight mechanisms.

  • Enterprise approach
  • Program development

International Standards: Study Guidance

This section reviews international standards and global cooperation in financial crime, focusing on FATF recommendations and international treaties that facilitate the sharing of information and the harmonization of anti-crime efforts worldwide among various law enforcement agencies.

  • FATF recommendations
  • Global cooperation

Asset Recovery: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on the tools and processes for asset recovery, including asset tracing and the legal procedures for forfeiture, which are used for reclaiming funds lost to criminal activities and fraud in various international jurisdictions.

  • Asset tracing
  • Recovery processes

Exam-Day Guidance

The exam consists of 135 questions to be completed within 240 minutes. Manage your time effectively across the 13 domains to ensure all sections are addressed. Ensure you are familiar with the exam interface and testing requirements before the start time.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The CFCS: Certified Financial Crimes Specialist exam is an assessment that consists of 135 questions in total, designed to test your knowledge across all 13 domains of financial crime and related regulatory compliance areas.

What is the passing score?

To successfully earn the certification, candidates must achieve a passing score of 88/135 on the exam, which demonstrates a level of proficiency in the required financial crime subject areas and professional investigative standards.

How long is the exam?

The exam has a total duration of 240 minutes, providing candidates with time to carefully read and answer each of the 135 questions presented during the testing session at an authorized facility or online.

Where can I find the current certification path?

For the most accurate and up-to-date information regarding the current certification path, requirements, and any potential updates to the exam structure, please check with ACFCS directly for guidance on your specific professional goals.
