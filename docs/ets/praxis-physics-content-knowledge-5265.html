What This Exam Validates

The Praxis Physics: Content Knowledge (5265) exam measures the knowledge and skills required for beginning secondary school physics teachers. Certified by ETS, this assessment covers six distinct domains, including mechanics, electricity, and modern physics. It evaluates conceptual understanding, analytical thinking, and problem-solving abilities through 125 selected-response questions, ensuring candidates possess the necessary subject matter expertise for classroom instruction.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is for individuals pursuing a teaching career in secondary school physics. Candidates have typically completed or nearly completed a bachelor’s degree program with appropriate coursework in physics and education to demonstrate their readiness for the classroom.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Analyzing motion and forces
  • Evaluating electrical circuits
  • Applying wave phenomena
  • Calculating thermodynamic processes
  • Interpreting atomic structures
  • Conducting scientific inquiry

How to Prepare

Review the test specifications to understand the 125 questions. Create a personalized study plan based on the six content domains. Practice with sample questions to become familiar with the computer-delivered format. Since calculators are prohibited, focus on conceptual understanding and problem-solving techniques. Use the provided tables of physical constants and conversion factors to guide your review of physics principles.

Domain Study Guidance

Mechanics: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers motion, forces, and energy in great detail. It focuses heavily on kinematics, dynamics, fluid mechanics, and conservation laws, including work, power, and rotational motion for candidates.

  • Newton's laws of motion
  • Conservation of energy and momentum
  • Fluid mechanics principles

Electricity and Magnetism: Study Guidance

This particular domain addresses electrostatics, circuits, and magnetism completely. It involves analyzing electric fields, potential, capacitance, and the complex interaction between electric and magnetic fields during the assessment.

  • Ohm's law and circuit analysis
  • Magnetic fields and induction
  • Capacitance and inductance

Optics and Waves: Study Guidance

This educational domain examines wave characteristics and optics extensively. It covers wave propagation, sound, the electromagnetic spectrum, and geometric optics including lenses and mirrors for future educators preparing for testing.

  • Wave phenomena and interference
  • Geometric optics and ray tracing
  • Doppler effect

Heat, Energy, and Thermodynamics: Study Guidance

This instructional domain focuses entirely on heat, energy, and thermodynamics. It includes temperature measurement, heat transfer mechanisms, phase transitions, kinetic molecular theory, and the fundamental laws of thermodynamics.

  • Laws of thermodynamics
  • Ideal gas laws
  • Heat transfer mechanisms

Modern Physics, and Atomic and Nuclear Structure: Study Guidance

This academic domain covers modern physics and atomic structure extensively. It addresses atomic models, radioactivity, nuclear processes, and advanced concepts like wave-particle duality and special relativity for candidates.

  • Atomic and nuclear structure
  • Radioactive decay and half-life
  • Modern physics concepts

Scientific Inquiry, Processes, and Social Perspectives: Study Guidance

This broad domain evaluates scientific inquiry, experimental design, and the social impact of physics. It covers data analysis, laboratory safety protocols, and the vital role of technology in society.

  • Experimental design and data analysis
  • Scientific inquiry processes
  • Impact of physics on the environment

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive with proper identification at your scheduled test center. You will have 150 minutes to complete the 125 questions. Use the provided tutorial to learn the computer interface before the timer begins. Calculators are not permitted during the test.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The Praxis Physics: Content Knowledge (5265) exam consists of exactly 125 selected-response questions. These questions comprehensively cover the six distinct domains of physics content and scientific inquiry for all candidates.

What is the passing score?

The passing score for the Praxis Physics: Content Knowledge (5265) exam varies by state or agency. You should confirm your specific requirements with your local licensing board or check with ETS for the current certification path.

How long is the exam?

The official exam is a timed 150-minute test. You are given this complete duration to answer all 125 selected-response questions provided on the computer-delivered assessment platform.

Are calculators allowed during the test?

No, calculator use is prohibited during the Praxis Physics: Content Knowledge (5265) exam. A periodic table and a table of physical constants and conversion factors are available as a Help screen.
