What This Exam Validates

The Praxis Social Studies Content Knowledge exam is designed by ETS for prospective secondary school social studies teachers. It covers six subject domains including United States history, world history, government, civics, geography, economics, and behavioral sciences. Candidates must answer 130 selected-response questions during a 120-minute computer-delivered test session to demonstrate their proficiency in these academic areas. The exam requires a deep understanding of historical events, political theories, and social science principles to meet the professional standards expected of educators in the field.

Who Should Take This Exam

Individuals seeking certification as a beginning teacher of social studies in secondary schools should take this exam. Candidates demonstrate content knowledge across history, civics, economics, geography, and behavioral sciences to meet professional standards for licensure.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • United States History
  • World History
  • Government/Civics/Political Science
  • Geography
  • Economics
  • Behavioral Sciences

How to Prepare

Review historical timelines, political theories, economic principles, and geographic data sources. Use practice questions and interactive tests to assess your understanding across all six domains. Check with ETS for the current certification path and official guidelines before your test date to ensure you are ready for the exam requirements and specific content expectations.

Domain Study Guidance

United States History: Study Guidance

This domain covers North American peoples prior to European colonization, major colonial developments, the American Revolution, the drafting of the Constitution, territorial expansion, sectionalism, the Civil War, industrialization, and major twentieth-century conflicts throughout the history of the United States.

  • North American geography and cultures prior to European colonization
  • Origins of the American Revolution and the United States Constitution
  • Major twentieth-century conflicts

World History: Study Guidance

This section examines classical civilizations, major transformations from invasions and trade, the Renaissance and Enlightenment periods, global interactions, revolutions, imperialism, and post-Cold War developments in world history throughout the examination period for all prospective social studies teachers.

  • Classical civilizations of Greece, Rome, Persia, India, and China
  • Political, social, and economic developments from the Renaissance through the Enlightenment
  • Global interactions and imperialism

Government/Civics/Political Science: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on political theory, major political theorists, political orientations, United States government institutions, constitutional underpinnings, civil liberties, comparative politics, and international relations across the selected-response items on the test to measure your grasp of civic structures.

  • Major political concepts, theorists, and orientations
  • United States government constitutional underpinnings and federalism
  • Civil liberties and comparative politics

Geography: Study Guidance

This section explores map types, spatial perspectives, mental maps, spatial patterns, geographic data sources, physical processes, ecosystems, human-environment interactions, natural resources, and cultural patterns evaluated in the computer-delivered test session to ensure proficiency in global geographic concepts.

  • Map types, projections, and spatial information analysis
  • Physical processes, climate patterns, and natural hazards
  • Human-environment interactions and natural resources

Economics: Study Guidance

This domain addresses microeconomics, scarcity, supply and demand, market efficiency, macroeconomics, measures of economic performance, monetary policy, fiscal policy, international trade, and economic growth tested across the various exam questions provided to assess your understanding of financial systems.

  • Microeconomic concepts including scarcity, opportunity costs, and market structures
  • Macroeconomic measures of performance, inflation, and unemployment
  • Monetary and fiscal policy

Behavioral Sciences: Study Guidance

This section analyzes how society and groups influence human behavior, the impact of culture, human adaptation, diversity, learning, personal identity, and psychological approaches across the testing period for all candidates seeking to demonstrate their knowledge of behavioral science.

  • Influence of society and groups on human behavior
  • Cultural change, human adaptation, and diversity
  • Psychological approaches and personal identity

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive at the testing center prepared for your 120-minute computer-delivered test containing 130 selected-response questions. Manage your testing time effectively across all six content categories to ensure you complete every section before the timer expires.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam contains exactly 130 selected-response questions distributed across six subject domains designed to test your knowledge of social studies content for secondary education and ensure you meet the required standards for professional licensure.

What is the passing score?

The passing score for the exam varies by state or agency. Candidates must check with their specific state certification requirements to determine the necessary score for licensure and professional practice in their region.

How long is the exam?

The exam has a strict duration of 120 minutes and is delivered via computer at designated testing centers. Plan your time accordingly to address all questions and complete the assessment within the allotted period.

What content categories are covered on the test?

The test covers United States History, World History, Government, Civics, Political Science, Geography, Economics, and Behavioral Sciences. These domains represent the core knowledge required for teachers to succeed in the classroom.
