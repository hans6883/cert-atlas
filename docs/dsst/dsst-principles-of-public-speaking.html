What This Exam Validates

The DSST Principles of Public Speaking examination evaluates fundamental knowledge and practical competencies in public communication. Administered and certified by DSST, this detailed 120-minute multiple-choice assessment covers eight distinct domains, including audience analysis, speech structure, delivery methods, and research strategies, enabling motivated learners to demonstrate college-level communication competence and earn valuable academic credit.

Who Should Take This Exam

Students seeking college credit for public speaking, military personnel utilizing DANTES benefits, and adult learners wishing to validate communication competence for academic progression or professional development should take this examination.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Ethical considerations
  • Audience analysis
  • Speech structure
  • Speech content
  • Research methods
  • Language and style
  • Public delivery

How to Prepare

Carefully review core concepts across all eight examination domains by studying public speaking textbooks, reviewing official guidelines, and utilizing detailed flashcards. Practice outlining informative and persuasive speeches, analyzing diverse target audiences, and refining your delivery techniques to meet testing standards and achieve a passing score of 400 points on the 100-question assessment.

Domain Study Guidance

Ethical, Social, and Theoretical Considerations of Public Speaking: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on ethical principles, important social responsibilities, and theoretical foundations that guide effective and responsible public speaking practices across various modern professional, civic, and academic communication environments today.

  • Ethical responsibilities
  • Theoretical foundations
  • Social considerations

Audience Analysis, Adaptation and Effect: Study Guidance

This domain centers on analyzing target audiences, adapting message content successfully to listener needs, and evaluating the overall communication effects on diverse groups of listeners during public presentations.

  • Audience analysis
  • Listener adaptation
  • Communication effects

Topics and Purposes of Speeches: Study Guidance

This domain examines selecting appropriate speech topics and determining clear, focused purposes for public presentations designed to inform, persuade, or entertain specific target audiences in varied professional settings.

  • Topic selection
  • Speech purposes
  • Informative and persuasive goals

Structure/Organization: Study Guidance

This domain covers the structural organization of speeches, including compelling introductions, well-developed main body paragraphs, logical transitions, and memorable conclusions that enhance message clarity, outline coherence, and overall audience comprehension during presentations.

  • Speech organization
  • Outlining methods
  • Transitions and structure

Content: Study Guidance

This domain deals with selecting, developing, and supporting central ideas with credible evidence, relevant visual aids, and diverse supporting materials to reinforce main arguments during public speaking engagements.

  • Idea development
  • Supporting materials
  • Visual aid usage

Research: Study Guidance

This domain addresses research techniques, gathering highly credible information from reliable sources, and citing those sources properly within speech preparation to maintain academic integrity and professional presentation standards.

  • Research techniques
  • Credible sources
  • Information gathering

Language and Style: Study Guidance

This domain examines precise language choice, appropriate stylistic devices, linguistic clarity, and tone suitable for various speaking occasions to ensure messages resonate effectively with intended listeners across different contexts.

  • Language choice
  • Stylistic devices
  • Tone and clarity

Delivery: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on practical delivery techniques, including vocal variety, physical movement, nonverbal communication cues, and effective strategies for reducing speech anxiety during live public presentations given by test takers.

  • Vocal delivery
  • Nonverbal communication
  • Stage presence

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive at the testing center early to complete check-in procedures for your computer-based examination. Manage your testing time carefully across all 100 questions during the 120 minutes allowed.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The examination consists of a total of exactly 100 multiple-choice questions that are delivered entirely via a computer-based testing platform at authorized test centers for all participating candidates.

What is the passing score?

To successfully pass this certification examination and earn college credit, test takers must achieve a minimum scaled score of 400 points across the multiple-choice questions evaluated on test day.

How long is the exam?

All candidates are given a total duration of 120 minutes to complete the multiple-choice assessment and answer all questions carefully before their scheduled testing session concludes.

How much does the exam cost?

The standard exam price is $85 for regular civilian candidates, while eligible military personnel can take the assessment completely free of charge through DANTES funding support.
