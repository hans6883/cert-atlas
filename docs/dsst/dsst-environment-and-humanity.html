Historical SS510 Scope

Environment and Humanity was a DSST exam covering ecosystems, population, land use, water, energy, pollution, and policy. This exam is now retired. The DSST program has replaced it with the Environmental Science exam. All candidates seeking credit for this subject area must now register for and complete the Environmental Science exam instead of this retired version to fulfill their academic requirements and demonstrate proficiency in these environmental topics.

Who SS510 Was For

This exam was intended for students seeking college credit for introductory environmental studies. Candidates should now focus on the Environmental Science exam, which serves the same academic purpose for students and military personnel seeking to validate their knowledge.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Analyzing ecosystem dynamics
  • Evaluating population growth impacts
  • Assessing land use management
  • Monitoring water resource quality
  • Reviewing energy production methods
  • Identifying pollution control strategies

How to Reuse Your Preparation

Focus your study efforts entirely on the Environmental Science exam. Much of the core content regarding ecosystems, pollution, and policy remains relevant. Review the official DSST exam guide for Environmental Science to align your study plan with current objectives. Use practice tests to identify gaps in your knowledge of energy and water resources, ensuring you are prepared for the 100 questions on the active exam. This approach ensures you are ready for the testing environment.

Historical Domain Guide

Ecosystems: Historical Scope

This domain covers the biological and physical interactions within ecosystems, focusing on how organisms and their environments function together to maintain balance and support life cycles within various habitats across the globe for all living species.

  • Ecosystem structure
  • Energy flow
  • Biogeochemical cycles

Population: Historical Scope

This domain examines the factors influencing human and animal population growth, distribution, and the resulting pressures on natural resources, including birth rates, death rates, and the impact of migration on local environments and global sustainability.

  • Growth rates
  • Demographic transitions
  • Carrying capacity

Land Use: Historical Scope

This domain addresses how land is utilized for agriculture, urban development, and conservation, including the environmental consequences of these choices and the long-term sustainability of various land management practices used by society to support human needs.

  • Land management
  • Resource allocation
  • Urban planning

Water Resources: Historical Scope

This domain covers the availability, quality, and management of water resources, including drinking water, wastewater, and watershed protection, while analyzing the importance of maintaining healthy aquatic ecosystems for human survival and the health of the planet.

  • Water quality standards
  • Aquatic ecosystems
  • Hydrologic cycle

Energy: Historical Scope

This domain focuses on energy sources, including both renewable and non-renewable options, and the environmental impacts of energy consumption, exploring how different power generation methods affect the climate and global resource availability for future generations of humans.

  • Energy efficiency
  • Alternative energy
  • Fossil fuels

Pollution: Historical Scope

This domain addresses the types, sources, and effects of air, water, and land pollution, along with methods for mitigation and cleanup, emphasizing the importance of reducing toxic waste to protect public health and the natural environment.

  • Pollutant types
  • Cleanup programs
  • Waste management

Environmental Policy: Historical Scope

This domain covers the legislative and regulatory frameworks, such as the Clean Water Act, that govern environmental protection and management, ensuring that human activities remain within legal boundaries to preserve natural ecosystems and protect public resources.

  • Environmental laws
  • Regulatory compliance
  • Policy implementation

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam consists of 100 multiple-choice questions. This format is used for the current Environmental Science exam, which replaced the retired version to ensure consistent testing standards for all candidates seeking to earn college credit for their knowledge.

What is the passing score?

The passing score for the exam is 400. This standard applies to the current DSST testing requirements and must be met to earn college credit for the subject, ensuring that all candidates demonstrate a sufficient level of academic understanding.

How long is the exam?

The exam duration is 120 minutes. This time limit is provided for the computer-based testing session, allowing candidates sufficient time to answer all questions accurately and provide their best possible responses during the allotted time for the exam.

What is the cost of the exam?

The exam price is $85 for civilians. It is free for eligible military personnel via DANTES, providing an accessible path for service members to earn college credit while maintaining the established standards for all test takers.
