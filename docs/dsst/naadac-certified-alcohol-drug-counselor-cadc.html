What This Exam Validates

The Substance Abuse exam, administered by DSST, measures knowledge equivalent to a lower-level baccalaureate college course. It covers substance abuse terminology, drug classifications, neurophysiological principles, and the effects of various substances including alcohol, tobacco, opioids, and hallucinogens. The exam also addresses treatment strategies, societal impacts, and the biological nature of addiction, providing an overview of substance use disorders and their management in clinical and social settings. This assessment evaluates how different substances influence brain chemistry and bodily functions during active use, while reviewing the history of various drug classes and their associated dependency risks across 14 distinct content domains.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is intended for students seeking college credit for their knowledge of substance abuse. It is appropriate for those who have studied addiction, pharmacology, or social work and wish to demonstrate proficiency in these areas through a standardized assessment.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Understanding substance abuse terminology
  • Classifying drugs by pharmacological effect
  • Analyzing neurophysiological principles
  • Evaluating alcohol dependency and treatment
  • Identifying effects of inhalants and stimulants
  • Assessing opioid and cannabinoid use

How to Prepare

Review textbooks such as Drugs and Society or Drug, Society and Human Behavior. Compare your current knowledge against the 14 content domains provided in the exam outline. Focus on understanding the pharmacological effects, withdrawal symptoms, and treatment options for each drug class. Practice with sample questions to familiarize yourself with the multiple-choice format and the specific terminology used in the exam. Ensure you are comfortable with the core concepts before testing to reach the required passing score of 400.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The Substance Abuse exam consists of 100 multiple-choice questions. You must answer these within the allotted time to demonstrate your knowledge of the subject matter and earn your college credit for the course.

What is the passing score?

To receive credit for the exam, you must achieve a minimum score of 400. This score is based on the evaluation of the test development process and content by the American Council on Education for academic credit.

How long is the exam?

The exam is a 120-minute assessment. You have two hours to complete all 100 questions, so pacing yourself throughout the various content domains is important for success on the day of your test at the center.

Where can I find the current certification path?

For information regarding the current certification path or any updates to the exam requirements, please check with DSST directly. They provide the most accurate and up-to-date details for all their testing programs and credit recommendations.
