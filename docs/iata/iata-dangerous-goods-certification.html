What This Exam Validates

The IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR) certification, administered by IATA, validates your ability to manage hazardous shipments. This exam covers the classification, packing, labeling, and documentation of dangerous goods for air transport. It ensures that personnel understand the regulatory requirements necessary to maintain safety and efficiency when handling, storing, and reporting dangerous goods in accordance with international standards. By achieving this certification, professionals demonstrate their commitment to safety and compliance within the aviation industry, ensuring that all hazardous materials are processed correctly and safely according to the established global standards for air transport.

Who Should Take This Exam

This certification is intended for airline staff, freight forwarders, ground handlers, and shippers. Professionals responsible for the classification, packing, or documentation of hazardous materials in air transport should take this exam to ensure compliance with international regulations and safety standards.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Understanding dangerous goods basics
  • Recognizing hazardous materials
  • Identifying undeclared goods in documentation
  • Reporting dangerous goods accidents
  • Verifying shipper declarations
  • Managing air waybill documentation

How to Prepare

Review the current IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations manual to understand the procedures. Focus on the classification of hazardous materials and the specific requirements for documentation and incident reporting. Practice identifying hidden dangerous goods within shipping documents. Ensure you are familiar with the 30 questions you will face during the 75-minute exam to manage your time effectively. Consistent study of the regulatory framework is required for success.

Understanding the basics of dangerous goods: Study Guidance

This domain covers the fundamental concepts and regulatory framework governing the transport of dangerous goods by air, including the specific responsibilities of shippers and operators to ensure safety and compliance throughout the entire logistics chain.

Recognizing dangerous goods: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on the identification and classification of hazardous materials, including explosives, gases, and toxic substances, to ensure safe handling and transport in accordance with the strict requirements set forth by international aviation authorities.

  • Classification of explosives and gases
  • Identification of toxic and corrosive materials

Indicators within documentation of undeclared dangerous goods: Study Guidance

This domain addresses the identification of undeclared dangerous goods within shipping documentation and the verification of air waybills and shipper declarations to prevent the accidental transport of prohibited or improperly labeled hazardous materials by air.

  • Shipper's declaration verification
  • Identifying hidden dangerous goods
  • Air waybill review

Report dangerous goods accidents: Study Guidance

This domain covers the procedures for reporting accidents and incidents involving dangerous goods, including document retention and information provision to ensure that all safety protocols are followed after any potential hazardous material event occurs.
