What This Exam Validates

The Principles and Practice of Surveying examination is the professional licensure exam for surveyors and is taken after the FS exam and the experience required by the candidate's licensing board. It is a closed-book computer-based test taken with an electronic reference handbook, and the specification covers professional survey practices, standards and specifications, business practices, the several areas of surveying practice, and legal principles. Legal principles carry the largest single share of the paper and a further block covers boundary practice specifically, so the exam is substantially a test of law as applied by a surveyor: common law boundary doctrine, conveyances, easements and unwritten rights sit alongside control networks, construction staking and topographic survey practice.

Who Should Take This Exam

The PS exam is for surveyors who have passed the FS exam and completed the experience their state board requires, and who are ready to take professional responsibility for boundary determinations and to seal survey plats and legal descriptions.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Common law boundary doctrine, evidence and unwritten rights
  • Conveyances, easements, rights of way and legal descriptions
  • ALTA/NSPS, FEMA and NGS standards and specifications
  • Control, construction, as-built and topographic survey practice
  • Survey business practice, liability and professional ethics

How to Prepare

Weight the study plan towards legal principles and boundary practice, which together dominate the paper, and study them as a surveyor applies them rather than as abstract law: work from evidence to conclusion the way a boundary resolution actually proceeds. Learn the ALTA/NSPS and FEMA standards precisely, because those questions turn on specific requirements rather than judgement and are reliably winnable. Use the PS Reference Handbook throughout preparation so its contents are familiar on screen, and rehearse the business practice and liability material, which candidates routinely under-prepare relative to its share of the exam.

The exam is closed book with the electronic handbook supplied on screen, and the identification requirements are those in the NCEES Examinee Guide. Plan the scheduled break rather than treating it as spare working time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the exam open book?

No. Every NCEES computer-based exam is closed book. An electronic reference handbook is supplied on screen at the test centre, and it is the only reference you may use, which is why candidates are advised to study from that handbook rather than from a textbook.

Where do the domain percentages come from?

NCEES publishes a range of questions per domain rather than a percentage. The percentages shown here are the midpoints of those published ranges, normalised across the domains so they total one hundred percent. The published question range is shown alongside each domain so you can see the original figure NCEES actually states.

How do I get the reference handbook before the exam?

NCEES makes the current reference handbook available to download from your MyNCEES account, and a printed edition of some handbooks is sold separately. The handbook is licensed for your personal use and may not be redistributed, so obtain it from NCEES directly rather than from a third party.

What identification do I need at the test centre?

The NCEES Examinee Guide sets out the identification, arrival and test centre rules that apply to every NCEES exam, including what you may bring and what happens if a test centre closes. Read the current edition before your appointment, because the requirements are enforced strictly.
