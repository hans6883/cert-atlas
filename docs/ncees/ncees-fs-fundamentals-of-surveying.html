What This Exam Validates

The Fundamentals of Surveying examination is the first step towards surveying licensure in the United States and is normally taken near the end of, or shortly after, an accredited surveying degree. It is a closed-book computer-based test of 110 questions in a six-hour appointment that includes the tutorial and an optional scheduled break, taken with an electronic reference handbook, and it uses both SI and US Customary units. The specification spans surveying and mapping methods, boundary law and real property principles, survey computations and computer applications, business concepts, and applied mathematics and statistics. Boundary law carries the single largest share, which surprises candidates who expect a purely technical paper: the FS tests legal reasoning about property alongside instrumentation and computation.

Who Should Take This Exam

The FS exam is designed for surveying students in their final year and recent graduates seeking the surveyor-intern credential, and for practising survey technicians beginning the path to professional licensure. No prior licence is required, and passing it is the prerequisite for the PS examination.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • GNSS, total station and levelling instrumentation and field procedure
  • Boundary law, PLSS, metes and bounds, and real property principles
  • Mapping, CAD, GIS and digital terrain modelling
  • Survey computations, adjustments and coordinate geometry
  • Applied mathematics, statistics and surveying business practice

How to Prepare

Treat boundary law and real property principles as a first-class technical subject rather than as background reading, because it carries the largest single share of the paper and it is where technically strong candidates most often lose marks. Work every computation type from the FS Reference Handbook so that the formulas and tables on screen during the exam are already familiar, and practise in both SI and US Customary units since the paper uses both and unit conversion errors are unforgiving. Cover the business concepts and statistics areas deliberately: they are small individually but together they represent a meaningful block of accessible marks.

Exam-Day Guidance

The six-hour appointment includes the tutorial and one optional scheduled break, so the working time is less than six hours and should be budgeted accordingly. Bring the identification named in the NCEES Examinee Guide; the exam is closed book with the handbook supplied on screen.

Frequently asked questions

Is the exam open book?

No. Every NCEES computer-based exam is closed book. An electronic reference handbook is supplied on screen at the test centre, and it is the only reference you may use, which is why candidates are advised to study from that handbook rather than from a textbook.

Where do the domain percentages come from?

NCEES publishes a range of questions per domain rather than a percentage. The percentages shown here are the midpoints of those published ranges, normalised across the domains so they total one hundred percent. The published question range is shown alongside each domain so you can see the original figure NCEES actually states.

How do I get the reference handbook before the exam?

NCEES makes the current reference handbook available to download from your MyNCEES account, and a printed edition of some handbooks is sold separately. The handbook is licensed for your personal use and may not be redistributed, so obtain it from NCEES directly rather than from a third party.

What identification do I need at the test centre?

The NCEES Examinee Guide sets out the identification, arrival and test centre rules that apply to every NCEES exam, including what you may bring and what happens if a test centre closes. Read the current edition before your appointment, because the requirements are enforced strictly.
