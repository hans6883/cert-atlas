What This Exam Validates

The FE Industrial Engineering Exam is expertly administered by NCEES as a computer-based test designed to evaluate aspiring engineering professionals across thirteen domains. These subjects include advanced mathematics, engineering sciences, probability, statistics, engineering economics, quantitative analysis, management, production systems, facilities, human factors, work design, quality control, and systems engineering. Examinees utilize the official reference handbook throughout the evaluation.

Who Should Take This Exam

Engineering students near graduation and professionals pursuing licensure should take this exam. Candidates demonstrate knowledge across industrial and systems engineering principles required by state boards.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Mathematics and analytic geometry
  • Engineering economics and cash flows
  • Probability distributions and regression
  • Linear programming and optimization
  • Supply chain management and logistics
  • Ergonomics and workplace safety
  • Quality control and Six Sigma
  • Reliability engineering and life-cycle

How to Prepare

Review the official NCEES reference handbook extensively before your scheduled exam day to ensure complete familiarity with all provided formulas. Practice solving industrial engineering problems using both metric and USCS units. Focus your study time diligently on high-weight areas like probability, statistics, manufacturing systems, engineering economics, quantitative analysis, modeling, and quality control.

Domain Study Guidance

Mathematics: Study Guidance

This specific mathematics domain thoroughly covers analytic geometry, calculus involving derivatives, integrals, progressions, and series, plus linear algebra operations and extensive vector analysis within the evaluation scope for all candidates.

  • Analytic geometry areas and volumes
  • Calculus derivatives and integrals
  • Matrix operations and vector analysis

Engineering Sciences: Study Guidance

This engineering sciences domain carefully addresses thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, statics, dynamics, material properties, and electricity for competency evaluation during the official testing appointment session administered by the board.

  • Thermodynamics and fluid mechanics
  • Statics, dynamics, and materials
  • Electricity and electrical circuits

Ethics and Professional Practice: Study Guidance

This professional practice section focuses heavily on codes of ethics, professional licensure requirements, legal agreements, contracts, legal responsibility, and public protection issues during actual engineering practice and professional employment.

  • Codes of ethics and licensure
  • Agreements and contracts
  • Public protection and regulatory issues

Engineering Economics: Study Guidance

This economic domain involves discounted cash flows, alternative evaluations, detailed cost analyses, various depreciation methods, and corporate taxes affecting financial decisions in complex engineering projects and capital investments.

  • Discounted cash flows and time value of money
  • Evaluation of alternatives like PW and IRR
  • Depreciation and taxes

Probability and Statistics: Study Guidance

This statistical domain covers discrete and continuous probabilities, statistical distributions, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, linear regression models, and systematic design of experiments applications used in analytical problem solving.

  • Probability distributions and functions
  • Estimation, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing
  • Design of experiments and ANOVA

Modeling and Quantitative Analysis: Study Guidance

This quantitative section focuses on data analytics, process flowcharts, linear programming methods, optimization models, queuing theories, and stochastic simulation techniques designed for evaluating complex engineering systems and operational networks.

  • Data, logic development, and analytics
  • Linear programming and optimization
  • Stochastic models and simulation

Engineering Management: Study Guidance

This broad management domain examines administrative principles, project management techniques, performance measurements, risk analysis, and strategic decision making for industrial engineering projects, professional teams, and modern enterprise operations.

  • Principles and tools of management
  • Project management scheduling tools like PERT and CPM
  • Decision making and risk under uncertainty

Manufacturing, Service, and Other Production Systems: Study Guidance

This vital production systems domain covers manufacturing processes, industrial production systems, forecasting methods, aggregate planning, operational scheduling, and continuous process improvements within modern industrial facilities and manufacturing plants.

  • Manufacturing processes and new technologies
  • Forecasting using moving average and exponential smoothing
  • Planning, scheduling, and lean systems

Facilities and Supply Chain: Study Guidance

This crucial facilities module involves facility layout planning, location analysis, manufacturing capacity analysis, and supply chain network design principles intended to achieve maximum operational efficiency and resource utilization.

  • Flow, layout, and location analysis
  • Capacity analysis and material handling
  • Supply chain management and design

Human Factors, Ergonomics, and Safety: Study Guidance

This safety domain addresses human factors engineering, user displays, machine controls, workplace safety standards, industrial hygiene, and physical ergonomics in industrial environments to protect workers from occupational hazards.

  • Human factors displays and controls
  • Safety and industrial hygiene workplace hazards
  • Ergonomics and biomechanics

Work Design: Study Guidance

This structured work design section focuses on methods analysis, workstation design, principles of motion economy, time study, work sampling, and learning curves for improving productivity in various industrial operations.

  • Methods analysis and workstation design
  • Work measurement and time study
  • Learning curves

Quality: Study Guidance

This quality domain covers quality management systems like Six Sigma and TQM, alongside statistical quality control methods and process capability measurements for manufacturing environments and service industry operations.

  • Quality management, planning, and assurance
  • Quality control control charts and sampling plans
  • Process capability

Systems Engineering, Analysis, and Design: Study Guidance

This complete systems engineering domain examines requirements analysis, system design, functional analysis, risk management, life-cycle engineering, and system reliability across various engineering projects and complex enterprise architectures.

  • Requirements analysis and system design
  • Risk management and FMEA
  • Reliability engineering and availability

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive prepared for a 360-minute computer-based test that includes 110 questions, a tutorial, and an optional break. Use the electronic PDF reference handbook provided during the exam.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam contains 110 questions administered via a computer-based format. Examinees have 360 minutes to complete the test, which includes a tutorial and an optional scheduled break.

What is the passing score?

NCEES does not publish a specific passing score for the exam. Scoring models use psychometric adjustments and scaling determined by NCEES; check with NCEES for current details.

How long is the exam?

The total appointment time allocated for this computer-based evaluation is exactly 360 minutes, which includes answering the 110 questions, reviewing an introductory software tutorial, and taking an optional scheduled break during the session.

What reference materials are allowed during the test?

The exam is closed book. An electronic PDF version of the FE Reference Handbook is provided within the computer-based testing interface for your use during the exam.
