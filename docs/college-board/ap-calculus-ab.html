What This Exam Validates

AP Calculus AB is a current, paper‑based assessment created by the College Board. It evaluates students’ mastery of limits, continuity, differentiation techniques, analytical and contextual applications, integration, differential equations and their practical uses. The exam consists of multiple‑choice and free‑response sections, lasts 195 minutes, and is scored on a 1–5 scale where a score of 3 is passing.

Who Should Take This Exam

High‑school juniors or seniors who have completed a year‑long pre‑calculus sequence and plan to pursue college‑level mathematics, engineering, physics, economics, or related STEM fields should take AP Calculus AB in preparation for college.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Limits and Continuity
  • Differentiation techniques
  • Analytical applications of differentiation
  • Integration and accumulation
  • Differential equations
  • Applications of integration

How to Prepare

Review the official Course and Exam Description, then study each domain using the OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 textbook. Practice multiple‑choice questions daily, and write timed free‑response solutions for past AP problems. Focus on the weighted domains—especially Integration (18.5 %) and Analytical Differentiation (16.5 %). Use College Board’s released practice exams to build stamina for the 195‑minute paper‑based format.

Domain Study Guidance

Limits and Continuity: Study Guidance

Limits and Continuity introduces the precise epsilon‑delta definition of a limit, explores limit laws, and examines continuity at individual points and across intervals, establishing the fundamental logical base for all subsequent calculus concepts.

  • limit definitions
  • epsilon‑delta proofs
  • continuity at a point

Differentiation: Definition and Fundamental Properties: Study Guidance

Differentiation: Definition and Fundamental Properties defines the derivative as a limit, presents basic differentiation rules such as power, product, quotient, and chain rules, and connects slopes to rates of change in real‑world contexts.

  • definition of derivative
  • basic differentiation rules
  • tangent line interpretation

Differentiation: Composite, Implicit, and Inverse Functions: Study Guidance

Differentiation: Composite, Implicit, and Inverse Functions covers techniques for differentiating composite functions using the chain rule, handling equations defined implicitly through implicit differentiation, and finding derivatives of inverse functions via reciprocal relationships.

  • chain rule
  • implicit differentiation
  • inverse function differentiation

Contextual Applications of Differentiation: Study Guidance

Contextual Applications of Differentiation applies derivative concepts to real‑world problems, including optimization of quantities, related‑rates scenarios involving changing variables, and motion analysis where velocity and acceleration are derived from position functions.

  • optimization problems
  • related rates
  • motion analysis

Analytical Applications of Differentiation: Study Guidance

Analytical Applications of Differentiation focuses on using first and second derivatives to sketch curves, determine concavity, locate inflection points, and apply the Mean Value Theorem for detailed analysis of function behavior.

  • curve sketching
  • concavity and inflection points
  • Mean Value Theorem

Integration and Accumulation of Change: Study Guidance

Integration and Accumulation of Change introduces definite integrals, explains the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus linking differentiation and integration, and teaches techniques for calculating area, accumulated change, and net growth over intervals.

  • definite integrals
  • Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
  • area under curves

Differential Equations: Study Guidance

Differential Equations covers solving basic separable first‑order equations, modeling exponential growth and decay processes, and constructing and interpreting initial‑value problems that illustrate how integration solves real‑world mathematical dynamic systems.

  • separable equations
  • growth and decay models
  • initial‑value problems

Applications of Integration: Study Guidance

Applications of Integration examines practical uses of definite integrals such as calculating volumes by slicing, determining arc length of curves, and solving work or fluid‑pressure problems that require accumulation of infinitesimal contributions.

  • volume by slicing
  • arc length
  • work and fluid pressure

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early with your admission ticket, a photo ID, and a #2 pencil. Bring a calculator that meets College Board specifications. Allocate time for both sections, double‑check free‑response answers, and stay hydrated.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The AP Calculus AB exam consists of a multiple‑choice section and a free‑response section. The exact number of questions is detailed in the official College Board exam guide and varies each year.

What is the passing score?

A score of 3 on the 1–5 AP scale is considered passing for AP Calculus AB, indicating qualified performance according to College Board standards.

How long is the exam?

The AP Calculus AB exam is administered on paper in a single session lasting 195 minutes. It consists of a multiple‑choice section followed by a free‑response section, each with its own time allocation.

How much does the AP Calculus AB exam cost?

The standard fee for the AP Calculus AB exam is $98 per student. Eligible students may qualify for a reduced fee of $55, and an additional $40 surcharge is added for international candidates.
