What This Exam Validates

The AP German Language and Culture exam, administered by the College Board, measures students’ proficiency in German across six required course themes. Delivered as a paper‑based test at schools in May, it combines multiple‑choice and free‑response sections within a 165‑minute session. Scores are reported on a 1–5 scale, and a score of 3 is generally regarded as passing.

Who Should Take This Exam

High school students enrolled in an AP German course who want college-level credit or placement should take this exam. Candidates should have working German skills in interpersonal, interpretive, and presentational communication.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Interpersonal writing and speaking in German
  • Interpretive reading of authentic texts and data
  • Interpretive listening to audio and audiovisual sources
  • Presentational writing with German diacritics
  • Cultural comparison across German-speaking societies
  • Argumentation and source-based essay writing
  • Use of authentic literary and informational materials

How to Prepare

Practice with authentic German audio, video, and written materials tied to each of the six themes. Type regularly in German, including diacritics such as ä, ö, ü, and ß. Build comfort with both timed multiple-choice passages and free-response writing and speaking tasks before the May administration.

Domain Study Guidance

Families in Different Societies: Study Guidance

Domain 1 examines families in different societies, focusing on community activities, family relationships, and urban and rural life in German‑speaking regions. Students explore family values, household structures, and caregiving patterns.

  • Community and family relationships
  • Urban and rural lifestyles
  • Demographic patterns in German-speaking regions

The Influence of Language and Culture on Identity: Study Guidance

Domain 2 investigates how language and culture shape identity, including dialect variation, heritage language use, and evolving linguistic practices. Students compare regional features and reflect on language change.

  • Regional dialects and pronunciation
  • Language change and identity
  • German in global and heritage contexts

Influences of Beauty and Art: Study Guidance

Domain 3 covers influences of beauty and art, including architecture, visual and performing arts, literature, and design. Students analyze how artistic works reflect and challenge cultural contemporary perspectives.

  • Visual and performing arts
  • Architecture and design
  • Cultural perspectives in literature and film

How Science and Technology Affect Our Lives: Study Guidance

Domain 4 explores how science and technology affect daily life, from social media habits to artificial intelligence and connected devices. Students evaluate benefits, risks, and broader societal impacts.

  • Social media and personal technology
  • Artificial intelligence and emerging tools
  • Societal impacts of innovation

Factors That Impact the Quality of Life: Study Guidance

Domain 5 addresses factors that impact quality of life, including education, career, entertainment, food, health, sports, travel, and daily routines. Students compare contemporary lifestyles and global habits across different cultures.

  • Education and career paths
  • Health, sports, and leisure
  • Travel, tourism, and entertainment

Environmental, Political, and Societal Challenges: Study Guidance

Domain 6 examines environmental, political, and societal challenges, including global communication, natural world topics, history, policy, and regional transportation. Students analyze complex current today’s issues facing German‑speaking communities.

  • Environmental and natural world issues
  • Political and policy challenges
  • Transportation and global communication

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive on time for the paper-based exam in May. Expect a 165-minute session with multiple-choice and free-response sections. Bring pencils and be ready to write and speak in German.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The AP German exam uses two formats: Multiple Choice and Free Response. The exam guide describes these two sections within a 165-minute paper-based test administered at school in May.

What is the passing score?

AP exams are scored on a 1–5 scale, and a score of 3 is generally considered passing. The College Board reports AP German results on this same 1–5 scale.

How long is the exam?

The AP German Language and Culture exam is 165 minutes long. It is a paper-based test with multiple-choice and free-response sections given at schools in May.

How much does the AP German exam cost?

The exam costs $98 per registration, with a reduced fee of $55 for students with fee reductions. An additional $40 international surcharge applies for students testing outside the United States.

What themes does the AP German exam cover?

The exam is organized around six required course themes: Families in Different Societies, The Influence of Language and Culture on Identity, Influences of Beauty and Art, How Science and Technology Affect Our Lives, Factors That Impact the Quality of Life, and Environmental, Political, and Societal Challenges.

Do I need to renew my AP German certification?

No renewal is required. The AP German Language and Culture exam score remains valid; for any long-term credential questions, check with College Board for current certification paths.
