What This Exam Validates

AP Physics C: Mechanics is an advanced College Board examination structured to fully evaluate your comprehensive understanding of classical mechanics and calculus-based physics principles. The rigorous assessment thoroughly covers kinematics, Newton's laws of motion, work, energy, power, systems of particles, linear momentum, rotation, oscillations, and gravitation across seven distinct topical domains.

Who Should Take This Exam

Students enrolled in advanced physics courses who have a background in calculus and classical mechanics should take this exam. It suits high school learners seeking college credit.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Kinematics analysis
  • Newton's Laws application
  • Work and energy calculations
  • Momentum conservation
  • Rotational dynamics
  • Oscillations study
  • Gravitation principles

How to Prepare

Carefully review the seven domain weightings and the official course framework provided by the College Board. Practice solving challenging free response and multiple choice problems under strict timed conditions to build your confidence. Utilize the official College Board exam guide and study documentation to focus your review on kinematics, mechanics principles, vector applications, and problem-solving strategies before test day.

Domain Study Guidance

Kinematics: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers kinematics in detail, accounting for 12.5 percent of the overall exam weight, while focusing specifically on motion descriptions, positional variables, and calculus applications.

  • Displacement, velocity, and acceleration
  • Motion in one and two dimensions

Newton's Laws of Motion: Study Guidance

Newton's Laws of Motion comprise 22.5 percent of the exam content, deeply emphasizing force vectors, dynamic equilibrium, and the complex mechanical interactions between physical bodies currently in motion.

  • Newton's three laws
  • Friction and circular motion dynamics

Work, Energy, and Power: Study Guidance

Work, Energy, and Power represent 20.0 percent of the overall examination, centering heavily on conservation laws, mechanical energy transfers, and the specific work-energy theorem calculations required by the board.

  • Work-energy theorem
  • Conservative forces and potential energy

Systems of Particles and Linear Momentum: Study Guidance

Systems of Particles and Linear Momentum make up 15.0 percent of the exam, focusing on collision theory, center of mass computations, and linear momentum conservation principles across various scenarios.

  • Impulse and momentum conservation
  • Center of mass calculations

Rotation: Study Guidance

Rotation covers advanced angular kinematics and rotational dynamics as part of the physics curriculum, dealing extensively with fixed axis rotation, rotational inertia, torque, and the conservation of angular momentum principles during testing.

  • Rotational inertia
  • Torque and angular momentum conservation

Oscillations: Study Guidance

Oscillations account for 12.5 percent of the exam, concentrating intensely on periodic motion, physical pendulums, simple harmonic motion, and complex spring-mass system behaviors evaluated during the assessment.

  • Simple harmonic motion
  • Spring-mass systems

Gravitation: Study Guidance

Gravitation addresses complex celestial mechanics and universal gravitational force laws operating between point masses and spherical bodies throughout space and orbiting satellites according to official course guidelines outlined by the board.

  • Newton's universal law of gravitation
  • Orbits of planets and satellites

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive at your school in May for this paper-based assessment. Manage your time across the 90-minute duration, ensuring you address both multiple choice and free response sections.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam is divided into two distinct sections containing multiple choice and free response questions administered on paper at your local school during the month of May.

What is the passing score?

The passing score for this exam is a 3 on a scale of 1 to 5, which often qualifies successful students for valuable college credit.

How long is the exam?

The total exam duration is exactly 90 minutes. It is a traditional paper-based test consisting of multiple choice and free response sections administered during the month of May.

What is the exam price?

The exam costs $98 per exam registration, featuring a reduced price of $55 for eligible students with fee reductions, plus a mandatory $40 international surcharge.
