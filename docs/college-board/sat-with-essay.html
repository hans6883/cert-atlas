Historical SAT Essay Scope

The SAT with Essay was a College Board assessment covering Reading and Writing, Math, and an Essay section. This exam is discontinued as of 2021 and is no longer offered to any candidates. Because the College Board has retired this exam and named no successor, candidates should consult the official College Board website for current testing requirements and available programs. The exam utilized a composite score scale of 400–1600 to measure student performance across its various sections, while the Essay section provided subscores on a scale of 6–24.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam format consisted of two primary sections: Reading and Writing, and Math. It utilized multiple choice and student-produced response question types. As the exam is retired, these specific counts are no longer applicable to current testing or available for student registration.

What is the passing score?

The composite score scale for the exam was 400–1600. Additionally, the Essay section utilized a subscore scale of 6–24, covering Reading, Analysis, and Writing. This exam is now discontinued, meaning these scores are no longer issued or recognized by the College Board.

How long is the exam?

The exam was a digital, multistage adaptive test delivered via the Bluebook app. Specific duration details are no longer relevant as the exam is retired and no longer offered by the College Board. Please check the official website for current testing information.

Is there a replacement for the SAT with Essay?

The College Board has retired the SAT with Essay and has not named a successor. Candidates should consult the official College Board website for information regarding current testing requirements and available programs that may better suit their specific academic goals and needs.

What was the testing format?

The exam was a digital, multistage adaptive test. Performance on the first module determined the difficulty of the second module. As the exam is retired, these technical details are provided for historical reference only and do not apply to current testing programs.
