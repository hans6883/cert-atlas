What This Exam Validates

AP Music Theory, offered by the College Board, evaluates students' understanding of pitch, scales, chords, harmonic progressions, musical form, and sight-singing. The paper-based exam lasts 180 minutes and includes multiple-choice and free-response sections. Scored on a 1–5 scale, a 3 is the minimum passing score. The fee is $98, with reductions available. Successful candidates may earn credit equivalent to a one- or two-semester introductory college music theory course.

Who Should Take This Exam

High-school students who can read and write musical notation, possess basic voice or instrument proficiency, and seek college credit for an introductory music theory course should enroll in AP Music Theory.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Identify pitch and key signatures
  • Construct major and minor scales
  • Analyze triads and seventh chords
  • Apply voice-leading rules
  • Recognize musical form and structure
  • Sight-sing notated melodies

How to Prepare

Begin by reviewing each of the nine domain outlines in the official course guide. Practice sight-singing daily, complete timed multiple-choice quizzes, and write out harmonic analyses on staff paper. Use AP Classroom resources and free online lessons such as musictheory.net to reinforce scales, chords, and voice-leading. Take full-length practice exams under timed conditions, then review incorrect items to target weak areas before the May test date.

Domain Study Guidance

Music Fundamentals I: Pitch, Major Scales and Key Signatures, Rhythm, Meter, and Expressive Elements: Study Guidance

Domain 1 covers fundamental pitch concepts, including staff notation, clefs, ledger lines, and the construction of major scales and key signatures. It also introduces basic rhythmic values, meter types, tempo markings, and expressive dynamics.

  • Pitch notation
  • Major scale construction
  • Key signature identification
  • Basic rhythmic values

Music Fundamentals II: Minor Scales and Key Signatures, Melody, Timbre, and Texture: Study Guidance

Domain 2 expands on minor scale forms—natural, harmonic, and melodic—while exploring key relationships, interval identification, melodic contour, and register. It also examines texture types such as monophony, homophony, and heterophony, plus syncopation.

  • Natural, harmonic, melodic minor scales
  • Key relationships
  • Interval identification
  • Texture types

Music Fundamentals III: Triads and Seventh Chords: Study Guidance

Domain 3 introduces diatonic triads and seventh chords, teaching students to identify chord qualities, construct inversions, and apply Roman-numeral analysis. Emphasis is placed on recognizing chord functions within common-practice harmony.

  • Triad identification
  • Seventh chord qualities
  • Chord inversions
  • Roman-numeral analysis

Harmony and Voice Leading I: Chord Function, Cadence, and Phrase: Study Guidance

Domain 4 focuses on chord function, cadence types, and four-part (SATB) voice leading in the style of the 18th century. Students learn proper chord spelling, doubling, voicing, and spacing conventions.

  • Chord function
  • Cadence identification
  • Four-part voice leading
  • Chord spelling conventions

Harmony and Voice Leading II: Chord Progressions and Predominant Chords: Study Guidance

Domain 5 examines predominant chords and their role in harmonic progressions. Learners analyze how these chords lead to dominant functions, study specific predominant types, and practice creating cadences that incorporate predominant function.

  • Predominant chord identification
  • Progression analysis
  • Cadence construction
  • Functional harmony

Harmony and Voice Leading III: Secondary Function: Study Guidance

Domain 6 introduces secondary functions, including secondary dominants and leading-tone chords, and the process of tonicization. Students learn how chords borrow from parallel keys to enrich harmonic progressions.

  • Secondary dominants
  • Leading-tone chords
  • Tonicization techniques
  • Borrowed chord applications

Harmony and Voice Leading IV: Extended and Altered Harmonies: Study Guidance

Domain 7 covers extended and altered harmonies, including ninth, eleventh, and thirteenth chords, as well as altered dominants. Learners practice identifying these chords and part-writing them within four-part textures.

  • Extended chord identification
  • Altered dominant chords
  • Thirteenth chord construction
  • Part-writing altered harmonies

Musical Form and Analysis: Study Guidance

Domain 8 explores musical modes, phrase relationships, and formal structures such as introductions, bridges, verses, choruses, codas, and codettas. Students analyze how these sections create larger forms and convey musical narrative.

  • Mode identification
  • Phrase relationships
  • Form sections
  • Structural analysis

Sight-Singing: Study Guidance

Domain 9 emphasizes sight-singing, requiring students to vocalize notated melodies accurately on first reading. Practice includes interval recognition, rhythmic accuracy, pitch maintenance, and interpretive phrasing within an appropriate range.

  • Sight-singing accuracy
  • Interval recognition
  • Rhythmic precision
  • Pitch control

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early with a photo ID, several #2 pencils, and an eraser. The paper-based test runs for 180 minutes, beginning with multiple-choice items followed by free-response questions. No calculators or electronic devices are permitted.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The AP Music Theory exam consists of a multiple-choice section and a free-response section; the exact number of items varies each year, but together they assess knowledge across all nine domains in the official course guide.

What is the passing score?

Scores are reported on a 1–5 scale; a score of 3 is the minimum passing level and may qualify students for college credit depending on the institution's policy. A score of 5 indicates the highest level of mastery.

How long is the exam?

The exam is a three-hour, 180-minute, paper-based assessment administered in May at the student's school under supervised conditions. No calculators, phones, or other electronic devices are permitted in the testing room.

How much does the exam cost?

The exam fee is $98 per student. Students who qualify for fee reductions pay $55, and an additional $40 international surcharge applies where relevant. Schools handle registration and payment through the College Board.

When is the AP Music Theory exam administered?

The AP Music Theory test is scheduled each May during the regular AP testing window; students take it at their school on the date set by the College Board. Specific dates appear on the official AP calendar annually.

Are there any exam codes I should know?

The course is identified as AP Music Theory and is administered by the College Board. There is no separate exam code beyond the standard AP designation used for registration and score reporting.
