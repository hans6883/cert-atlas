What This Exam Validates

AP Microeconomics, administered by the College Board, is a 135-minute paper-based exam taken at high schools each May. It tests how individual markets and economic agents behave, organized into six weighted content domains. Candidates complete a multiple-choice section and a free-response section, receiving composite results on a 1–5 scale the following July.

Who Should Take This Exam

High school students seeking college credit in economics, particularly those comfortable with graphs, algebra-based models, and analyzing firm and consumer decisions. The exam suits learners completing a first course in microeconomics.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Basic economic concepts and scarcity
  • Supply, demand, and market equilibrium analysis
  • Production costs and perfect competition models
  • Imperfect competition including monopoly and oligopoly
  • Factor markets for labor and capital
  • Market failure and government policy responses

How to Prepare

Begin with domain weightings to prioritize study time, allocating more hours to Production, Cost, and the Perfect Competition Model (23.5%) and Supply and Demand (22.5%). Practice free-response graphing daily, and time multiple-choice sets against the 135-minute limit. Review past College Board samples to learn scoring rubrics.

Domain Study Guidance

Basic Economic Concepts: Study Guidance

This 13.5% weighted domain covers Basic Economic Concepts including scarcity, opportunity cost, and the distinctions between microeconomics and macroeconomics. It sets the vocabulary used throughout the exam. Core models also appear here.

  • Define scarcity and opportunity cost clearly
  • Differentiate microeconomics from macroeconomics
  • Apply the production possibilities frontier

Supply and Demand: Study Guidance

Weighted at 22.5%, Supply and Demand forms the analytical core of the exam. Candidates work with market equilibrium, elasticity, and shifts caused by changes in determinants of supply or demand. Graphing appears frequently in free-response items.

  • Draw and interpret supply and demand graphs
  • Calculate price and income elasticities
  • Predict equilibrium changes from demand or supply shifts

Production, Cost, and the Perfect Competition Model: Study Guidance

At 23.5%, this is the heaviest domain. It covers production functions, short-run and long-run costs, and the perfect competition model including profit maximization and shutdown decisions. Calculations of marginal cost and average cost are central.

  • Derive a firm's cost curves from production data
  • Apply MR=MC to find profit-maximizing output
  • Identify short-run shutdown and long-run exit conditions

Imperfect Competition: Study Guidance

Imperfect Competition carries 18.5% of the exam weight. Topics include monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, and game theory. Candidates compare pricing, output, and efficiency against perfect competition benchmarks. Price discrimination and barriers to entry appear often.

  • Calculate monopoly price and output from MR=MC
  • Compare monopolistic competition to perfect competition
  • Apply simple game theory to oligopoly pricing

Factor Markets: Study Guidance

Factor Markets accounts for 11.5% of the score. The domain addresses derived demand, marginal revenue product, and wage determination in competitive and monopsonistic labor markets. Rent, interest, and profit are also treated as factor payments.

  • Apply MRP = MFC to determine hiring
  • Explain how derived demand shifts with product price
  • Distinguish economic rent from transfer earnings

Market Failure and the Role of Government: Study Guidance

Market Failure and the Role of Government carries 10.5% weight. Candidates analyze externalities, public goods, and common-pool resources alongside corrective taxes, subsidies, and regulation. Efficiency loss from market failure is a recurring free-response topic.

  • Distinguish positive from negative externalities
  • Evaluate Pigouvian taxes and subsidies graphically
  • Identify public goods and free-rider problems

Exam-Day Guidance

Bring a watch, several sharpened pencils, and a government-issued photo ID to your school's exam room. Pace yourself across the 135 minutes: budget the multiple-choice section and reserve time to graph clearly on each free-response.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam uses two question types: multiple choice and free response. Specific counts are set by College Board each year, so check the official AP Microeconomics course description for the current section breakdown and timing.

What is the passing score?

AP Microeconomics is scored on a 1–5 scale. A score of 3 is generally considered passing, though individual colleges set their own credit and placement policies for scores of 3, 4, or 5.

How long is the exam?

AP Microeconomics is a 135-minute paper-based exam administered at the student's school in May. It includes two timed sections covering multiple-choice questions and free-response problems.

How much does the exam cost?

The College Board charges $98 per AP Microeconomics exam. Students with fee reductions pay $55, and an additional $40 international surcharge applies for exams taken outside the United States.

What topics are on AP Microeconomics?

Six domains make up the exam: Basic Economic Concepts (13.5%), Supply and Demand (22.5%), Production, Cost, and the Perfect Competition Model (23.5%), Imperfect Competition (18.5%), Factor Markets (11.5%), and Market Failure and the Role of Government (10.5%).

Is AP Microeconomics still offered?

Yes. AP Microeconomics is a current College Board program. It is administered each May at participating high schools. For long-term certification or credit transfer questions, check with College Board.
