CBA Certified Business Appraiser has been retired

NACVA's CBA/MCBA page states the closure: 'Thus, in 2016, NACVA suspended its activities relating to the proliferation and further development of the CBA/ MCBA, and thereby the credential is no longer available to interested candidates. We have also dissolved the non-profit IBA.' Existing holders keep the designation ('To continue carrying the CBA/MCBA, designees must comply with the same recertification requirements imposed on NACVA's CVA/MAFF/ABAR designees.'). The page names no successor; NACVA's live credentials are the CVA, MAFF and ABAR.

No direct replacement is named in the reviewed official sources.

Historical CBA Certified Business Appraiser Scope

The CBA Certified Business Appraiser certification is managed by NACVA. This exam evaluates professional competency across nine distinct domains, ranging from engagement planning and qualitative analysis to complex valuation methodologies, cost of capital concepts, and the application of discounts and premiums. It ensures practitioners demonstrate the technical knowledge required to perform business valuations in accordance with established professional standards and industry requirements.

Who CBA Certified Business Appraiser Was For

This certification is intended for experienced professionals who perform business valuations. Candidates should possess a background in financial analysis, valuation methodology, and the application of professional standards to business interests across a wide variety of professional contexts.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Engagement planning and acceptance
  • Qualitative and quantitative analysis
  • Valuation approach selection
  • Cost of capital methodology
  • Discounts and premiums application
  • Professional standards compliance

How to Reuse Your Preparation

Review the NACVA Body of Knowledge and the candidate handbook to understand the nine exam domains. Focus your study efforts on the 26.0% weight assigned to valuation approaches and the 17.5% weight allocated for cost of capital concepts. Practice applying professional standards to case scenarios and ensure you are familiar with the reporting guidelines outlined in the official documentation provided by NACVA.

Historical Domain Guide

Overview: Historical Scope

This domain provides an overview of the business valuation field, establishing the concepts and the general framework necessary for professional practice within the industry for all candidates seeking to demonstrate their knowledge of the valuation process.

  • Valuation overview
  • Practice scope

NACVA/GACVA Professional Responsibilities and Standards: Historical Scope

This domain addresses the professional responsibilities and ethical standards required by NACVA and GACVA, ensuring that all practitioners maintain the level of integrity expected throughout their valuation engagements and interactions with clients and other professional stakeholders.

  • Professional conduct
  • Ethical standards

Engagement Acceptance and Planning: Historical Scope

This domain focuses on the initial steps of a valuation engagement, including the formal acceptance process and the planning required to execute a successful and compliant business valuation assignment for a variety of different client business entities.

  • Engagement acceptance
  • Assignment planning

Qualitative Analysis: Historical Scope

This domain covers the various qualitative factors that influence the value of a business interest, including business history, industry outlook, and other non-financial elements that impact the final valuation conclusion reached by the appraiser during the process.

  • Qualitative analysis techniques
  • Business history and outlook

Quantitative Analysis: Historical Scope

This domain focuses on the quantitative methods used to analyze financial data for valuation purposes, covering financial statement analysis and the modeling techniques required to derive accurate business value conclusions based on the available financial information provided.

  • Financial statement analysis
  • Quantitative modeling

Valuation Approaches: Historical Scope

This domain covers the three generally accepted valuation approaches and their practical application, which accounts for 26.0% of the exam, ensuring candidates can effectively utilize asset-based, market, and income-based valuation methods in their professional valuation reports.

  • Asset-based approach
  • Market approach
  • Income approach

Cost of Capital Concepts and Methodology, and Other Pricing Models: Historical Scope

This domain addresses the methodologies for determining the cost of capital and other pricing models, representing 17.5% of the exam content, which is used for calculating accurate discount rates and valuation multiples during the valuation engagement process.

  • Cost of capital concepts
  • Pricing model application

Discounts, Premiums, and Other Adjustments: Historical Scope

This domain covers the application of discounts and premiums to valuation conclusions, which accounts for 10.0% of the exam, focusing on marketability discounts and control premiums that adjust the final business value based on specific ownership interest characteristics.

  • Marketability discounts
  • Control premiums

Special Purposes and Circumstances: Historical Scope

This domain addresses valuation requirements for special purposes and unique circumstances, ensuring that appraisers are prepared to handle non-standard business valuations that require knowledge and specific adjustments to standard methodology for unique business entities or specific situations.

  • Special purpose valuations
  • Unique business circumstances

Frequently asked questions

How many domains are covered in the exam?

The exam covers nine distinct domains, ranging from an overview of valuation practice to specific methodologies like quantitative analysis, cost of capital, and the application of discounts and premiums, ensuring an assessment of your professional valuation skills.

What is the weight of the valuation approaches domain?

The valuation approaches domain is the most significant section of the exam, accounting for 26.0% of the total content weight, which requires candidates to have an understanding of asset-based, market, and income valuation methodologies.

Who is the certifying body for this exam?

The CBA Certified Business Appraiser certification is administered by NACVA, which is responsible for setting the professional standards and the body of knowledge required for all candidates seeking this professional designation.

Where can I find the current certification path?

For information regarding the current certification path and any updates to the program requirements, please check with NACVA directly to ensure you have the accurate and up-to-date guidance for your professional development.
