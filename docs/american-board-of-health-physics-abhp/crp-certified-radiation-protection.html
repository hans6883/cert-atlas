What This Exam Validates

The Certified Health Physicist Examination is professionally certified by the American Board of Health Physics. It tests professional knowledge across five domains, including measurements and instrumentation, standards and requirements, hazards analysis and controls, operations and procedures, and fundamentals and education to ensure safety and competence for all health physics professionals seeking this credential.

Who Should Take This Exam

Health physicists and radiation safety professionals seeking professional certification. Candidates should prepare for an evaluation covering instrumentation, regulatory compliance, hazard controls, operational procedures, and fundamental health physics education to demonstrate their expertise in the field.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Measurements and Instrumentation
  • Standards and Requirements
  • Hazards Analysis and Controls
  • Operations and Procedures
  • Fundamentals and Education

How to Prepare

Successful candidates spend considerable time studying for the certification examination. Review health physics fundamentals and applied aspects across all specialty areas. Joining a formal or informal study group helps maintain a systematic review schedule. Candidates should utilize official practice materials and textbooks to reinforce their understanding of radiation safety principles and regulatory frameworks before attempting the final assessment.

Domain Study Guidance

Measurements and Instrumentation: Study Guidance

This domain covers measurements and instrumentation, focusing on the selection and use of measuring instruments, data quality objectives, quality control, calibration, maintenance, and performance testing of instrumentation used in radiation protection settings.

  • Types of Measurements
  • Selection of Instruments

Standards and Requirements: Study Guidance

This domain covers standards and requirements, addressing guidelines from established groups like ICRP, NCRP, ANSI, and ASTM, alongside the specific requirements of regulatory agencies such as NRC, DOE, EPA, DOT, OSHA, and FEMA.

  • Maintenance of Licenses, Permits and Regulations
  • History and Development

Hazards Analysis and Controls: Study Guidance

This domain covers hazards analysis and controls, focusing on hazard identification, engineered controls, potential failures of protective control systems, and evaluating potential radiological consequences to ensure safety in diverse work environments.

  • Hazard Identification
  • Evaluate and Assess Significance/Consequence

Operations and Procedures: Study Guidance

This domain covers operations and procedures, incorporating radiation protection considerations into an operating program, standard operating practices, and effective emergency response measures to maintain safety during all routine and non-routine facility activities.

  • Standard Operating Practices and Procedures
  • Emergency Response

Fundamentals and Education: Study Guidance

This domain covers fundamentals and education, including the content of training the health physicist receives and the educational programs prepared, reviewed, and presented to staff members within the radiation protection department.

  • Skills of the Trade
  • Types

Exam-Day Guidance

Part I testing centers provide a computer-based calculator program. Calculators are allowed for the Part II exam. No recording or transmitting devices are permitted during the 540 minutes of testing.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam structure is detailed in official guidelines provided by the organization. Candidates should review the complete exam blueprint directly from the official American Board of Health Physics website to understand the exact format of the testing parts.

What is the passing score?

The passing score for Part I requires at least 95 questions correctly answered. For Part II, the passing score is 67.0 percent or better, which corresponds to 469 points out of a total possible 700 points.

How long is the exam?

Candidates are given a total of 540 minutes during the testing process for certification. This duration accommodates the thorough evaluation required to assess professional knowledge and competencies in health physics standards.

What are the domains of practice?

The exam covers five domains: Measurements and Instrumentation at 25.0 percent, Standards and Requirements at 20.0 percent, Hazards Analysis and Controls at 20.0 percent, Operations and Procedures at 20.0 percent, and Fundamentals and Education at 15.0 percent.
