Historical CFE-Law Scope

The CFE-Law exam, administered by the ACFE, covered legal elements of fraud, criminal and civil law, rights of suspects, evidence, and bankruptcy. This exam is officially retired as of 2026-06-02. The ACFE has not named a successor exam. Candidates seeking the CFE credential should consult the official ACFE website for the most current certification requirements and examination paths to ensure they are following the latest professional standards for the industry.

Who CFE-Law Was For

This exam was intended for professionals pursuing the Certified Fraud Examiner credential. It required knowledge of legal frameworks, evidence handling, and civil and criminal procedures relevant to fraud investigation. Candidates should verify current requirements with the ACFE.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Defining legal elements of fraud
  • Distinguishing criminal and civil law
  • Managing rights of suspects
  • Handling evidence procedures
  • Applying contract and bankruptcy law

How to Reuse Your Preparation

Because this exam is retired and has no successor, do not prepare for this specific content. Focus on current ACFE certification requirements instead. Review the official ACFE certification page to understand the updated path to the CFE credential. Any previous study of legal elements or evidence remains useful for general professional knowledge, but you must align your current efforts with the latest ACFE guidance to maintain compliance with current certification standards.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions were on the exam?

The retired CFE-Law exam consisted of exactly 125 questions. These questions were designed to test the candidate's understanding of legal principles related to fraud investigation, though this exam is no longer available for registration or completion by any certification candidates.

What was the passing score?

A 75% passing score was required for this exam. This percentage represented the standard threshold for demonstrating proficiency in the legal concepts covered, but please note that this requirement is no longer applicable as the exam has been officially retired by the ACFE.

How long was the exam?

The exam had a duration of 135 minutes. This time limit was established to allow candidates sufficient opportunity to answer all questions within the allotted period, though this duration is no longer relevant due to the retirement of the CFE-Law examination.

Is there a successor to this exam?

No, the ACFE has not named a successor to the retired CFE-Law exam. Please consult the official ACFE website for the most current certification information and to understand the updated requirements for obtaining your professional credential in the field of fraud examination.
