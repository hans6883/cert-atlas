OSHA 30-Hour Construction Safety Credential

OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration)

Not an examination

OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration) does not offer an examination for OSHA 30-Hour Construction Safety: it is a training programme. No exam blueprint, domain weights, exam code or practice test apply. Verified on the issuer's site September 19, 2026. issuer page

What This Exam Validates

The OSHA 30-Hour Construction Safety certification, provided by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration, covers safety and health standards for construction work. It addresses hazard identification, site management, and regulatory requirements. This 1800-minute program ensures participants understand their rights, employer responsibilities, and safety protocols for various construction environments, including fall protection, electrical safety, and material handling procedures. The training provides a foundation for maintaining safe work sites.

Who Should Take This Exam

This certification is intended for construction workers, site supervisors, and safety managers who need an understanding of site safety. It is suitable for individuals responsible for maintaining regulatory compliance and preventing workplace injuries on construction projects.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • OSHA regulatory compliance
  • Safety management systems
  • Hazard identification
  • Personal protective equipment
  • Construction health hazards
  • Stairway and ladder safety

How to Prepare

Review the regulations to understand the legal framework for construction safety. Focus on the Focus Four hazards and the requirements for personal protective equipment. Utilize the official OSHA exam guide to align your study with the required domains. Practice identifying hazards in common construction scenarios and understand the application of safety standards in real-world site conditions to ensure you are prepared for the assessment.

Domain Study Guidance

Domain 1: Study Guidance

This domain provides an introduction to the basic structure, mission, and purpose of OSHA regulations within the construction industry to ensure all workers understand the legal framework governing their daily work activities.

  • OSHA mission and purpose
  • Worker rights and employer responsibilities

Domain 2: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on the management of safety and health programs on construction sites, emphasizing the implementation of safety protocols and proactive hazard prevention strategies for all personnel working on the project.

  • Safety program implementation
  • Hazard prevention strategies

Domain 3: Study Guidance

This domain addresses the four primary causes of fatalities in the construction industry, providing knowledge on how to identify and mitigate risks associated with common site-specific dangerous work activities and hazardous conditions.

  • Fall protection
  • Struck-by hazards
  • Caught-in or between hazards
  • Electrocution prevention

Domain 4: Study Guidance

This domain covers the selection, maintenance, and proper use of personal protective equipment for various construction tasks to ensure that workers remain shielded from potential site-related physical injuries during their daily operations.

  • Head and eye protection
  • Respiratory protection
  • Foot protection

Domain 5: Study Guidance

This domain identifies health hazards encountered during construction activities, focusing on the communication of chemical risks, the management of toxic substances, and the mitigation of harmful noise exposure levels for all site workers.

  • Hazard communication
  • Toxic and hazardous substances
  • Noise exposure

Domain 6: Study Guidance

This domain details the safety requirements for stairways and ladders on construction sites, ensuring that all access equipment is inspected, maintained, and used according to established industry safety standards for worker protection.

  • Ladder inspection and use
  • Stairway safety standards

Domain 7: Study Guidance

This domain covers elective topics relevant to construction safety needs, allowing participants to explore hazard mitigation techniques and safety protocols tailored to unique project environments and specific site conditions encountered by workers.

  • Site-specific safety topics
  • Hazard mitigation

Domain 8: Study Guidance

This domain includes optional topics that supplement the construction safety curriculum, providing additional safety training and regulatory guidance to enhance the competency of construction safety professionals working on various project sites.

  • Additional safety training
  • Regulatory guidance

Exam-Day Guidance

Ensure you have a stable internet connection for the 1800-minute course. Have your identification ready and be prepared to engage with the material across all 8 domains. Manage your time effectively to complete all sections and review your answers before final submission.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the exam?

The OSHA 30-Hour Construction Safety program is a 1800-minute course. You should plan your schedule carefully to accommodate this total duration for completion, ensuring you have enough time to finish all required modules without any interruption during the training.

What is the certifying body for this exam?

The certification is provided by OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration), which is the federal agency that sets the safety and health regulations for the construction industry to protect workers on the job site.

How many domains are covered in the training?

The training is structured into 8 distinct domains, ranging from the introduction to OSHA and safety management to specific hazards like stairways, ladders, and toxic substances, providing an overview of construction safety standards.

Where can I find the current certification path?

For information regarding the current certification path or any updates to the program requirements, check with OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration) for the most accurate and up-to-date guidance on maintaining your professional safety credentials.
