OSHA 30-Hour General Industry Safety Credential

OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration)

Not an examination

OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration) does not offer an examination for OSHA 30-Hour General Industry Safety: it is a training programme. No exam blueprint, domain weights, exam code or practice test apply. Verified on the issuer's site September 20, 2026. issuer page

What This Exam Validates

The OSHA 30-Hour General Industry Safety program covers workplace safety and health standards across 10 distinct domains to ensure proper protocols. Certified by OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration), this 1800-minute course provides detailed training focused on recognizing, avoiding, abating, and preventing various workplace hazards in general industry settings to ensure full compliance.

Who Should Take This Exam

This program is intended for safety directors, foremen, and field supervisors with safety responsibility. Personnel seeking extensive training in workplace hazards and general industry standards will benefit from this course.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Introduction to OSHA
  • Managing Safety and Health
  • Walking and Working Surfaces
  • Exit Routes and Fire Protection
  • Electrical Safety
  • Personal Protective Equipment
  • Materials Handling
  • Hazard Communication

How to Prepare

Review all training materials provided across the 10 domains to ensure complete understanding of the subject matter. Focus heavily on standard definitions, hazard identification methods, and specific safety requirements. Allocate sufficient study time to complete the 1800-minute duration and ensure complete comprehension of workplace safety regulations before attempting any final evaluations.

Domain Study Guidance

Domain 1: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers the background, history, purpose, and overall role of OSHA in workplace safety and health enforcement as well as worker rights within general industry environments.

  • OSHA history and mission
  • Worker rights and employer responsibilities

Domain 2: Study Guidance

This particular domain focuses heavily on the implementation, execution, and administration of effective safety and health management systems within the daily workplace environment to reduce injuries across operations.

  • Safety program elements
  • Hazard identification processes

Domain 3: Study Guidance

This section details the regulations regarding walking-working surfaces, ladders, floor openings, and the necessary fall protection systems required for workers operating in elevated positions across industrial facilities safely.

  • Surface conditions and loads
  • Ladder safety and fall protection

Domain 4: Study Guidance

This domain examines specific requirements for emergency exit routes, action plans, detailed fire prevention plans, and overall active fire protection systems designed for facilities to ensure safety consistently.

  • Exit route maintenance
  • Fire prevention strategies

Domain 5: Study Guidance

This technical area addresses electrical safety standards, proper wiring methods, equipment design principles, and necessary protection against dangerous electrical hazards found in workplaces during operations without exception.

  • Electrical work practices
  • Equipment safeguarding

Domain 6: Study Guidance

This module outlines the selection criteria, proper use, and routine maintenance requirements for personal protective equipment across various hazardous tasks performed by industrial workers throughout each assigned shift.

  • PPE hazard assessment
  • Equipment maintenance

Domain 7: Study Guidance

This domain covers safe procedures for materials handling, storage practices, safe usage methods, and proper disposal of hazardous and heavy items across industrial operations and storage facilities properly.

  • Safe lifting practices
  • Materials storage rules

Domain 8: Study Guidance

This section explains hazard communication standards, chemical labeling protocols, and safety data sheet requirements for maintaining chemical safety in the general industry workplace during all shifts and operations.

  • Chemical classification
  • Safety data sheets

Domain 9: Study Guidance

This elective domain provides additional topics to further customize safety training based on specific operational needs and unique workplace risks identified by facility supervisors and managers across sites.

  • Additional safety topics
  • Task-specific training

Domain 10: Study Guidance

This optional domain includes additional training modules to address supplementary general industry safety and health subjects for enhanced professional development of facility workers across industrial plants safely.

  • Optional safety modules
  • Supplemental practices

Exam-Day Guidance

Complete all required course modules systematically. Manage your time effectively across the 1800-minute duration to ensure thorough coverage of all 10 domains before finishing the training program.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the exam?

The OSHA 30-Hour General Industry Safety program has a total duration of 1800 minutes, which is carefully structured to provide detailed training across all required safety domains.

How many domains are covered?

The program covers a total of 10 domains, ranging from an introduction to OSHA and managing safety to electrical standards, personal protective equipment, and hazard communication.

Who certifies the training program?

The training program is officially certified by OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration), ensuring full compliance with federal safety and health standards for general industry workplaces.
