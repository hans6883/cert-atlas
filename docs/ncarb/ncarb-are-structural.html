Historical ARE SF Scope

The ARE SF exam, certified by NCARB, covered structural theory, load paths, material selection, and lateral systems. This exam is officially retired as of 2018-06-30. It has been replaced by the ARE-5 exam. Candidates seeking structural certification must now follow the current NCARB path, as the ARE SF exam is no longer available for testing or credit toward professional licensure. The transition ensures that all candidates meet modern standards for architectural practice and structural safety through the current successor program.

Who ARE SF Was For

Architects and design professionals pursuing licensure should focus on the current ARE-5 requirements. This retired exam is not for current candidates, who must instead meet the standards set by the successor program to demonstrate their professional competency and readiness for practice.

Historical Domain Guide

Structural Theory and Analysis: Historical Scope

This domain covers the fundamental principles of structural theory and the analytical methods used to determine how various building structures behave under different environmental conditions and loads, ensuring that candidates understand the core mechanics required for safe and effective architectural design.

  • Static equilibrium
  • Structural mechanics

Load Paths and Forces: Historical Scope

This domain addresses the identification of complex load paths and the calculation of forces acting upon structural elements within a building system, helping candidates master the evaluation of force resultants and support conditions necessary for maintaining overall structural integrity and stability.

  • Force resultants
  • Support conditions

Material Properties and Selection: Historical Scope

This domain focuses on the physical characteristics of building materials and the specific criteria used to select them for various structural applications, ensuring that architects can make informed decisions regarding material properties and the performance of individual structural elements in practice.

  • Material properties
  • Structural elements

Lateral and Vertical Load Systems: Historical Scope

This domain examines the design and function of systems that manage lateral and vertical loads to ensure building stability, covering the analysis of determinate planar structures and statically indeterminate systems to maintain safety standards across diverse building types and configurations.

  • Determinate planar structures
  • Statically indeterminate systems

Sustainability and Resilience: Historical Scope

This domain covers the application of sustainability and resilience principles to structural design and building performance, focusing on elastic stability and approximate methods to ensure that modern structures are both environmentally responsible and capable of withstanding long-term operational stresses.

  • Elastic stability
  • Approximate methods

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The retired ARE SF exam consisted of 95 multiple-choice questions and 2 case studies. This structure was designed to test a candidate's ability to apply structural knowledge in both theoretical and practical scenarios during the testing process.

What is the passing score?

NCARB does not publish specific passing scores for its exams. Candidates are evaluated based on their overall performance across the various domains, ensuring that they demonstrate the necessary competency required for professional licensure in the field of architecture.

How long is the exam?

The retired ARE SF exam had a total duration of 210 minutes. This time limit was established to allow candidates sufficient opportunity to complete the 95 multiple-choice questions and the associated case study sections within the testing environment.

What is the price of the exam?

The price for the retired ARE SF exam was $235. This fee covered the costs associated with the administration of the computer-based testing format, which included both the multiple-choice questions and the required case study components for all candidates.
