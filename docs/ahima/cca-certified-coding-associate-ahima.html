What This Exam Validates

The Certified Coding Associate exam is administered by AHIMA to verify professional competence in medical coding. It covers six domains, including clinical classification systems, reimbursement methodologies, health records, data content, compliance, information technologies, and confidentiality. This certification demonstrates a candidate's ability to accurately assign codes and manage health information across various healthcare settings, ensuring that data is handled with the level of professional integrity and technical accuracy required by the industry.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is designed for individuals seeking to demonstrate their proficiency in medical coding. It is suitable for entry-level coders or those transitioning into health information management roles who want to validate their technical knowledge and coding accuracy through a recognized professional credential.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Assigning inpatient and outpatient codes
  • Managing reimbursement methodologies
  • Analyzing medical record documentation
  • Ensuring regulatory compliance
  • Utilizing health information technology
  • Maintaining patient privacy

How to Prepare

Review the official AHIMA exam guide to understand the structure of the 105 questions. Focus your study on the six domains, specifically practicing code assignment and reimbursement methodologies. Use the provided coding guidelines to familiarize yourself with current conventions. Ensure you are comfortable with the 120-minute time limit by taking practice assessments to build your speed and accuracy before the actual test date.

Domain Study Guidance

Clinical Classification Systems: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on the interpretation of healthcare data and the application of coding guidelines for inpatient, outpatient, and physician settings. Candidates must demonstrate proficiency in applying standard classification systems to ensure accurate documentation and billing across diverse clinical environments and patient care scenarios.

  • Inpatient coding guidelines
  • Outpatient coding guidelines

Reimbursement Methodologies: Study Guidance

This domain covers the sequencing of codes for reimbursement, the assignment of DRGs and APCs, and the management of claim denials and medical necessity. Mastery of these methodologies is for ensuring that healthcare facilities receive appropriate payment for services rendered to patients.

  • DRG assignment
  • APC assignment

Health Records and Data Content: Study Guidance

This domain involves the retrieval, assembly, and analysis of medical records for completeness and data accuracy. Professionals must be capable of performing both quantitative and qualitative reviews to ensure that all patient information is correctly abstracted and maintained within the health information system.

  • Medical record retrieval
  • Quantitative analysis

Compliance: Study Guidance

This domain addresses ethical coding practices, the validation of codes against documentation, and preparation for external audits. It emphasizes the importance of maintaining standards of integrity when assigning codes and communicating with physicians to clarify documentation for accurate health record reporting.

  • Ethical coding
  • Physician query

Information Technologies: Study Guidance

This domain covers the use of electronic health records, encoding software, and various information technology tools. Candidates are expected to understand how to navigate digital systems efficiently to support coding workflows and ensure that technology is utilized to enhance the accuracy of health data.

  • EHR navigation
  • Encoding software

Confidentiality and Privacy: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on the protection of patient information, privacy education, and the secure handling of electronic and physical records. It is for coders to understand legal requirements and organizational policies that govern the confidentiality of sensitive health information in all settings.

  • Patient confidentiality
  • Privacy violations

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive prepared to complete 105 questions within 120 minutes. Manage your time effectively across the six domains to ensure you reach the passing score of 300. Check with AHIMA for the current certification path and specific testing center requirements to ensure a smooth experience.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The CCA exam consists of 105 questions that test your knowledge across six distinct domains of health information management and medical coding. These questions are designed to evaluate your practical application of coding standards and your ability to manage health records effectively in a professional healthcare environment.

What is the passing score?

To earn the Certified Coding Associate designation, candidates must achieve a passing score of 300 on the exam. This score reflects the level of competency required to perform entry-level coding tasks accurately and ethically within the healthcare industry while maintaining compliance with all relevant national standards.

How long is the exam?

The exam is a 120-minute test. Candidates must complete all 105 questions within this duration. It is recommended that you practice time management during your study sessions to ensure you can answer all questions before the time expires at the testing center.

Where can I find the current certification path?

For information regarding the certification path or updates to the program, please check with AHIMA directly for the most current details. They provide the official guidance on requirements, maintenance of your credential, and any changes to the examination structure that may occur over time.
