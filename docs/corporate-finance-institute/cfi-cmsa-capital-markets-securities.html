What This Exam Validates

The CMSA Capital Markets and Securities Analyst certification offered by the Corporate Finance Institute measures core analytical knowledge across eight distinct subject domains. This qualification validates fundamental competencies in analyzing equities, derivatives, fixed income instruments, corporate valuation, commodities, portfolio risk management, trade operations workflows, and foreign exchange markets for modern financial market professionals.

Who Should Take This Exam

Finance professionals, analysts, and traders seeking validation of their market knowledge. Candidates should focus on mastering financial instruments, trading mechanics, and quantitative risk metrics to succeed in this certification path.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Equities valuation
  • Derivatives pricing
  • Fixed income analysis
  • Commodities trading
  • Risk management metrics
  • Foreign exchange analysis

How to Prepare

Review the domain weightings carefully and allocate preparation time proportionally across every section. Focus heavily on core subjects like equities and derivatives, which carry 20% weights each, alongside fixed income and risk management at 15% each. Practice core calculation problems repeatedly and verify your understanding against the 70% passing score requirement.

Domain Study Guidance

Equities: Study Guidance

This domain covers equity securities, corporate market structures, and valuation methods. Candidates examine public equity instruments, market mechanics, trading environments, and standard techniques used to analyze share prices and overall company equity performance across diverse capital markets.

  • Equity instruments
  • Market mechanics

Derivatives: Study Guidance

This domain examines derivative contracts, instrument pricing models, and hedging strategies. Candidates explore options, futures, forward commitments, and swaps, learning how market participants utilize these derivative contracts to manage financial risk, hedge portfolio exposures, and execute speculative market trades.

  • Derivative pricing
  • Hedging strategies

Fixed Income: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on fixed income securities, debt instruments, bond pricing principles, and yield measures. Preparation requires mastering bond valuation methodologies, coupon structures, yield curves, credit spreads, and the interest rate dynamics that affect debt portfolios in capital markets.

  • Bond valuation
  • Yield calculations

Valuation: Study Guidance

This domain addresses fundamental valuation techniques and financial modeling principles used in professional asset analysis. Candidates study comparative multiples, intrinsic cash flow modeling, financial statement drivers, and standard quantitative metrics applied when determining asset worth across global capital markets.

  • Valuation models
  • Financial metrics

Commodities: Study Guidance

This domain explores commodity markets, contract structures, physical assets, and global supply chain pricing. Study focus centers on understanding commodity futures, physical trading mechanics, market seasonality, pricing drivers, and storage factors that shape energy, agricultural, and metals trading environments.

  • Commodity futures
  • Pricing dynamics

Risk Management: Study Guidance

This domain investigates risk management frameworks, market exposures, volatility measurement, and portfolio mitigation techniques. Candidates learn to assess market, credit, and operational risks while exploring hedging strategies, risk controls, and quantitative metrics necessary to safeguard investment assets against unexpected market fluctuations.

  • Risk exposure
  • Mitigation techniques

Trade Operations: Study Guidance

This domain covers trade operations, order execution lifecycle, clearing mechanisms, and settlement workflows. Candidates study middle and back office processes, regulatory compliance considerations, trade confirmation protocols, transaction processing systems, and settlement cycles necessary for running smooth financial market operations.

  • Settlement cycles
  • Operational workflows

Foreign Exchange: Study Guidance

This domain analyzes foreign exchange markets, major currency pairs, exchange rate determinants, and cross-currency valuations. Preparation emphasizes currency trading mechanics, spot and forward exchange agreements, macroeconomic drivers, interest rate parity concepts, and techniques used to manage corporate currency risks.

  • Currency pairs
  • Cross-rate calculations

Exam-Day Guidance

Manage your time effectively across all eight domains during the examination session. Ensure you meet the 70% passing score threshold by reviewing all calculated answers carefully before final submission.

Frequently asked questions

What is the passing score?

The passing score for the CMSA Capital Markets and Securities Analyst certification exam is 70%. Candidates must meet or exceed this percentage threshold to successfully earn the credential.

Who administers the CMSA certification?

The Corporate Finance Institute administers and certifies candidates for the CMSA Capital Markets and Securities Analyst credential. Candidates earn this professional designation after completing the designated curriculum requirements and meeting the assessment standard set by the organization.

How many domains are covered on the exam?

The exam covers 8 distinct domains: Equities, Derivatives, Fixed Income, Valuation, Commodities, Risk Management, Trade Operations, and Foreign Exchange. Each domain is weighted individually to ensure a balanced assessment of capital markets knowledge across all operational and analytical areas.

What are the highest weighted domains on the test?

The highest weighted domains are Equities and Derivatives, which account for 20% each of the exam curriculum. Following these core sections are Fixed Income and Risk Management, which are each assigned a 15% weighting, representing key priorities during candidate study.
