Scheduled retirement

EPPP - Examination for Professional Practice in Psychology retires on March 31, 2028

ASPPB is replacing the EPPP (Part 1-Knowledge) and EPPP (Part 2-Skills) with a single integrated EPPP: 'The current EPPP (Part 1-Knowledge) and EPPP (Part 2-Skills) will remain available to all approved candidates until the integrated EPPP takes effect in the fourth quarter of 2027. At that time, the EPPP (Part 1-Knowledge) and EPPP (Part 2 - Skills) will no longer be available to first-time candidates.' Retakes run through March 31, 2028; 'Effective on April 1, 2028, the integrated EPPP will be the only exam - the EPPP - accessible to all candidates.' The integrated EPPP is beta-tested Spring 2027 and operational from Fall 2027.

No direct replacement is named in the reviewed official sources.

What This Exam Validates

The Examination for Professional Practice in Psychology is owned by the Association of State and Provincial Psychology Boards and utilized by state boards and provincial colleges. It evaluates the knowledge and skills required to practice psychology independently through multiple-choice questions across 8 distinct content domains that define the standards for professional practice. This assessment ensures that all candidates possess the knowledge necessary to provide safe, effective, and ethical psychological services to the public within their respective jurisdictions.

Who Should Take This Exam

Psychology applicants seeking licensure and certification for independent practice. Candidates typically hold an appropriate doctoral degree in psychology along with the necessary supervised professional experience required by their specific jurisdiction to qualify for the examination process.

How to Prepare Before Retirement

Review the eight content areas defined by the official Job Task Analysis documentation. Register through Certemy once formally approved by your licensing authority. Ensure you understand test specifications and practice answering multiple-choice questions under timed conditions within the Pearson VUE testing network to prepare for the 255-minute duration of the exam. Consistent study of these domains is required for success.

Domain Study Guidance

Biological bases of behavior: Study Guidance

This domain covers the biological bases of human behavior, accounting for 10.0 percent of the total exam content and emphasizing core neuroanatomy, physiological mechanisms, and associated biological processes necessary for professional practice in the field of psychology.

  • Neuroanatomy and neurochemistry
  • Physiological psychology
  • Biological processes

Cognitive-affective bases of behavior: Study Guidance

This section focuses on cognitive and affective bases of behavior, representing 13.0 percent of the examination questions while exploring complex learning mechanisms, memory processes, emotion theories, and human motivation as they relate to the practice of psychology.

  • Learning and memory processes
  • Emotion and motivation theories
  • Cognitive mechanisms

Social and cultural bases of behavior: Study Guidance

This area addresses social and cultural bases of behavior, weighted at 11.0 percent of the test, and evaluates candidate comprehension regarding social psychology principles, interpersonal interactions, and the role of cultural diversity and context in clinical settings.

  • Social psychology principles
  • Cultural diversity and context
  • Interpersonal interactions

Growth and lifespan development: Study Guidance

This domain examines growth and lifespan development, making up 12.0 percent of total exam content by evaluating various developmental stages across the entire human lifespan along with significant milestones, transitions, and psychological changes relevant to professional practice.

  • Developmental stages across the lifespan
  • Lifespan milestones and changes
  • Psychological transitions

Assessment and diagnosis: Study Guidance

This section evaluates assessment and diagnosis, constituting 16.0 percent of the exam questions by testing psychological testing methods, clinical evaluation strategies, and standard diagnostic classification systems used by licensed professionals in their daily clinical work.

  • Psychological testing methods
  • Diagnostic classification systems
  • Clinical evaluation strategies

Treatment, intervention, prevention, and supervision: Study Guidance

This domain covers treatment, intervention, prevention, and supervision, accounting for 15.0 percent of the exam content and requiring knowledge of psychotherapeutic interventions, clinical supervision practices, and evidence-based prevention strategies used by licensed psychologists.

  • Psychotherapeutic interventions
  • Supervision practices
  • Prevention strategies

Research methods and statistics: Study Guidance

This content area focuses on research methods and statistics, representing 7.0 percent of the examination by measuring candidate understanding of experimental design, methodological approaches, and statistical analysis techniques applied in psychological research and clinical practice.

  • Experimental design and methodology
  • Statistical analysis techniques
  • Research application

Ethical, legal, and professional issues: Study Guidance

This section addresses ethical, legal, and professional issues, weighted at 16.0 percent of the test, to ensure candidates understand professional codes of conduct, ethical standards, and legal regulations governing the practice of psychology in their jurisdiction.

  • Professional codes of conduct
  • Legal regulations in psychology
  • Ethical standards

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive at the Pearson VUE testing center well before your scheduled appointment time with the required identification documents matching your Authorization to Test name. No scheduled breaks are provided during the 255-minute duration of the examination session, so please plan your personal needs accordingly before the test begins.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The examination consists of 225 total items. These items are designed to evaluate your professional knowledge and readiness for independent practice within your specific jurisdiction, ensuring that all candidates meet the required standards for providing safe and effective psychological services to the public.

What is the passing score?

The passing requirement is a 500 scaled score for independent practice or a 450 scaled score for supervised practice. These specific thresholds are determined by your individual state or provincial licensing board to ensure that all practitioners meet the necessary professional standards.

How long is the exam?

Candidates are given a total duration of 255 minutes to complete the test appointment. This time allocation includes the initial tutorial, the actual exam items, and a post-exam survey, so candidates should manage their time effectively throughout the entire session.

How do I register for the exam?

You must first apply for licensure through your state or provincial licensing authority. Once approved, you will complete the registration workflow and pay fees via Certemy before scheduling your appointment with Pearson VUE. Ensure all your documentation is accurate to avoid any delays in the process.
