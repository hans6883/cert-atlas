What This Exam Validates

The ABPP Neuropsychology Board Examination is administered by the American Board of Professional Psychology to assess professional competence in clinical neuropsychology. Candidates are evaluated during a 180-minute test session that covers 5 core domains. These 5 domains include Domain 1 (General Psychology including statistics and methodology), Domain 2 (General Clinical Psychology), Domain 3 (General Psychopathology/Neuropathology), Domain 4 (Brain-Behavior Relationships), and Domain 5 (Practice of Clinical Neuropsychology). Demonstrating expertise across all 5 specialized domains is required for specialty board certification.

Who Should Take This Exam

This examination is designed for licensed clinical psychologists who have completed formal post-doctoral training in clinical neuropsychology. Candidates seeking specialty board certification must demonstrate advanced theoretical knowledge and practical competence in brain-behavior relationships and clinical practice.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • General psychology including statistics and methodology
  • General clinical psychology principles
  • General psychopathology and neuropathology
  • Brain-behavior relationships analysis
  • Practice of clinical neuropsychology

How to Prepare

Candidates preparing for this 180-minute examination should systematically review all 5 core content domains outlined by the American Board of Professional Psychology. Study plans should begin with Domain 1 research methodology and statistics before progressing through clinical psychology, neuropathology, brain-behavior relationships, and professional practice. Allocating dedicated preparation time to each of the 5 domains ensures balanced mastery of the required material.

Domain Study Guidance

General Psychology (including statistics and methodology): Study Guidance

Domain 1 assesses knowledge in general psychology, focusing heavily on statistics and methodology. Candidates must understand how to apply quantitative methods, psychometric principles, and experimental designs to evaluate research data and clinical literature accurately within specialty clinical neuropsychology practice.

  • Statistical analysis
  • Research methodology

General Clinical Psychology: Study Guidance

Domain 2 covers general clinical psychology principles, evaluating candidate grasp of assessment techniques, psychological intervention strategies, and clinical decision-making. Practitioners must demonstrate proficiency in applying established psychological concepts and evidence-based diagnostic procedures across diverse clinical populations in various professional care settings.

  • Clinical assessment
  • Psychological interventions

General Psychopathology/Neuropathology: Study Guidance

Domain 3 focuses on general psychopathology and neuropathology. Candidates must identify neurological conditions, understand underlying neuropathological disease processes, and analyze corresponding psychological symptoms to ensure accurate diagnostic clarification and informed treatment planning for patients presenting with complex neurodevelopmental or acquired brain disorders.

  • Neuropathological conditions
  • Psychopathological symptoms

Brain-Behavior Relationships: Study Guidance

Domain 4 examines brain-behavior relationships and functional neuroanatomy. Candidates are evaluated on their ability to map cognitive, emotional, sensory, and motor impairments to specific neuroanatomical structures, neural networks, and pathological central nervous system conditions encountered in clinical practice.

  • Functional neuroanatomy
  • Brain-behavior mapping

Practice of Clinical Neuropsychology: Study Guidance

Domain 5 addresses the practice of clinical neuropsychology, focusing on professional standards, legal guidelines, and ethical considerations. Candidates must demonstrate readiness to conduct comprehensive evaluations, interpret complex diagnostic findings, and maintain exemplary standards of ethical care throughout their professional careers.

  • Professional ethics
  • Neuropsychological practice

Exam-Day Guidance

The ABPP Neuropsychology Board Examination is administered in a single 180-minute test session. Candidates must manage their time effectively across all 5 content domains during the 180 minutes to ensure every section is thoroughly addressed.
