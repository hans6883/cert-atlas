Historical ACNS-BC Scope

The Adult-Gerontology Clinical Nurse Specialist Board Certified (ACNS-BC) exam is a retired credential previously certified by the American Nurses Credentialing Center. This specific credential has been discontinued and replaced by the AGCNS-BC, which serves as the current, active pathway for all practitioners seeking professional certification in this clinical field. Candidates should note that the retired exam consisted of 175 multiple-choice questions and required a passing score of 350 to achieve the credential.

Who ACNS-BC Was For

Registered nurses who are currently pursuing clinical nurse specialist credentials should register for the active AGCNS-BC successor examination rather than attempting to pursue this retired credential, as the ACNS-BC is no longer available for new candidates to complete or earn.

How to Reuse Your Preparation

Direct all study efforts toward the AGCNS-BC successor examination. While core clinical knowledge transfers directly, ensure you review current ANCC guidelines and material specific to the active successor test rather than attempting to prepare for this retired exam format. Candidates should focus on the updated requirements and the 210 minutes allotted for the current testing process to ensure full readiness for the certification.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions were on the exam?

The retired ACNS-BC examination was a linear test that contained exactly 175 multiple-choice questions. Candidates were expected to demonstrate their clinical knowledge across these items to meet the certification standards established by the ANCC at that time.

What was the passing score?

The passing score for the retired ACNS-BC credential required a minimum scaled score of 350. This score was derived from a total scale ranging from 200 to 500, which was used to determine the competency of the nursing candidates.

How long was the exam?

Candidates were provided with a total duration of 210 minutes to complete the linear examination. This time limit was designed to allow sufficient opportunity for nurses to answer all questions while maintaining the integrity of the testing process.

What is the successor to the ACNS-BC exam?

The verified successor to the retired credential is the AGCNS-BC, which stands for the Adult-Gerontology Clinical Nurse Specialist Board Certification Examination. This exam is currently certified by the ANCC and serves as the primary pathway for certification.

What was the cost of the exam?

The retired ACNS-BC examination had a non-member price of 520 dollars. This fee structure was established by the ANCC to cover the administrative and testing costs associated with the certification process for all eligible nursing candidates.
