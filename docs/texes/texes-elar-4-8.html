Prepare for 217: English Language Arts and Reading 4–8 (217)

Historical TExES-117 Scope

The TExES English Language Arts and Reading 4–8 (117) was a professional certification exam administered by TExES. It covered five distinct domains, including oral language, reading development, and writing. This exam is officially retired as of 2021-12-31. The official successor is the English Language Arts and Reading 4–8 (217) exam. Candidates must now focus their professional efforts on the specific requirements and content of the 217 exam to achieve their state certification goals for teaching these subjects.

Who TExES-117 Was For

This exam was intended for educators seeking certification to teach English language arts and reading in grades 4–8. Current candidates must pursue the 217 successor exam to meet state certification requirements for this grade level and subject area.

Historical Domain Guide

Oral Language and Listening: Historical Scope

This domain covers the development of oral language and listening skills, emphasizing how spoken communication supports literacy and vocabulary growth in students. It provides a framework for understanding how verbal interaction facilitates the acquisition of complex language structures and listening comprehension abilities.

  • Oral language development
  • Listening comprehension strategies

Reading Development and Foundational Skills: Historical Scope

This domain focuses on reading development, including phonics, decoding, and the relationship between spoken sounds and written letters in the English language. It examines the foundational skills required for students to become fluent readers who can accurately interpret various types of printed text.

  • Phonics instruction
  • Decoding strategies
  • Word analysis

Reading Comprehension and Analysis: Historical Scope

This domain addresses reading comprehension and analysis, focusing on how students make sense of texts, analyze meaning, and apply active reading strategies. It emphasizes the cognitive processes involved in evaluating literature and informational texts to derive deeper meaning and synthesize information effectively.

  • Comprehension strategies
  • Text analysis
  • Literary evaluation

Written Language: Historical Scope

This domain covers written language, including the mechanics of writing, personal storytelling, and persuasive writing techniques across the curriculum. It highlights the importance of teaching students to organize their thoughts, use proper grammar, and communicate ideas clearly through various forms of written expression.

  • Writing strategies
  • Spelling and mechanics
  • Grammar usage

Research and Inquiry: Historical Scope

This domain involves research and inquiry skills, focusing on how students gather information and use evidence to support their learning and writing. It covers the systematic process of identifying research questions, locating credible sources, and integrating findings into coherent and well-supported academic projects.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The retired TExES-117 exam consisted of 100 questions in total. Candidates were expected to complete these within the allotted time frame to demonstrate their proficiency in English language arts and reading pedagogy for the specified grade levels.

What is the passing score?

The passing score for the TExES-117 exam was 240. This score represented the minimum level of competency required for certification in the state of Texas for educators teaching English language arts and reading in grades 4–8.

How long is the exam?

The TExES-117 exam had a duration of 150 minutes. During this time, candidates were required to answer all 100 questions provided in the computer-based format to meet the requirements for their professional teaching certification in the subject area.

What was the cost of the exam?

The price for the TExES-117 exam was $116. This fee covered the administration of the computer-based test and was a standard requirement for all candidates pursuing their initial teacher certification in the state of Texas for this specific field.
