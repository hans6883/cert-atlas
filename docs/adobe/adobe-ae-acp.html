What This Exam Validates

The Adobe Certified Professional After Effects exam, coded ACP-AfterEffects, is administered by Adobe through Certiport or Pearson VUE. It tests five domains of motion graphics and visual effects practice, from setting project requirements through rendering and exporting. Candidates complete 40 questions in a linear, computer-based format. The exam validates professional competency in industry-standard workflows.

Who Should Take This Exam

Motion designers, video editors, and graphic designers who already use After Effects for compositing, animation, and effects work should consider this exam. It suits practitioners seeking a credential that confirms working knowledge of the application.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Setting project requirements for motion graphics work
  • Understanding the After Effects interface and workflow
  • Working with layers, keyframes, and animation
  • Applying effects and managing color
  • Rendering and exporting finished compositions

How to Prepare

Build a study plan that mirrors the five exam domains, spending time in After Effects on each in turn. Practice performance-based tasks in a real project file rather than only reading. Review Adobe's official study documentation and the After Effects User Guide for current feature references before sitting the exam.

Domain Study Guidance

Setting Project Requirements: Study Guidance

This domain covers the planning stage of a motion graphics project, including interpreting briefs, identifying deliverables, and organizing assets before opening After Effects. It frames the technical work that follows in later domains.

  • Reading project briefs and client requirements
  • Identifying required deliverables and formats
  • Organizing source assets before production

Understanding After Effects: Study Guidance

This domain covers the After Effects environment itself, including workspaces, compositions, projects, and importing footage. It establishes the working context in which all later animation, effects, and rendering tasks take place.

  • Navigating workspaces and panels
  • Creating and configuring compositions
  • Importing and interpreting footage items

Working with Layers and Animation: Study Guidance

This domain covers layers, keyframes, motion tracking, and animation techniques inside After Effects. It includes selecting and arranging layers, working with 3D layers, and animating shape paths and masks.

  • Managing layers and layer properties
  • Setting and editing keyframes
  • Applying motion tracking and keying

Working with Effects and Color: Study Guidance

This domain covers applying effects, animation presets, and color adjustments to compositions. It includes color management, color correction effects, and the broad effect categories listed in the After Effects User Guide.

  • Applying effects and animation presets
  • Managing color and color correction
  • Using OpenColorIO and ACES workflows

Rendering and Exporting: Study Guidance

This domain covers rendering and exporting finished work from After Effects, including H.264 encoding, multi-frame rendering, and exporting still-image sequences. It also covers automated and network rendering workflows.

  • Configuring render queue and output settings
  • Encoding with H.264 and other codecs
  • Exporting still images and sequences

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive rested for a 50-minute linear exam with 40 questions. Read each performance-based task carefully before acting, and pace yourself so the final questions receive full attention.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam contains 40 questions delivered in a linear, computer-based format. Question types include multiple choice and performance-based tasks, the latter being unique to Adobe Certified Professional exams.

What is the passing score?

Candidates need a score of 66 to pass. The exam is scored on a fixed scale, and the passing threshold is set by Adobe for the ACP-AfterEffects certification.

How long is the exam?

The exam runs for 50 minutes from start to finish. Candidates should plan their pacing across all 40 questions within that single sitting to ensure completion before time expires.

How much does the exam cost?

The standard price is $180 when scheduled through Certiport or Pearson VUE. Pricing is set by Adobe and the test delivery partners, and may vary by region.

Does the certification need to be renewed?

Yes, renewal is required to keep the Adobe Certified Professional credential active. For the long-term renewal path, check with Adobe for the current certification cycle.

What topics does the exam cover?

The exam covers five domains: Setting Project Requirements, Understanding After Effects, Working with Layers and Animation, Working with Effects and Color, and Rendering and Exporting. Each domain groups related objectives tested in the question pool.
