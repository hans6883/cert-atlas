What This Exam Validates

CAS Exam 1: Probability for Casualty Actuaries is a professional certification exam administered by the Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS). It evaluates a candidate's ability to apply probability tools to quantitatively assess risk in actuarial science. The exam covers general probability, univariate random variables, and multivariate random variables, requiring a strong command of calculus and knowledge of insurance and risk management concepts to succeed in this professional field.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is intended for individuals pursuing a professional career as a casualty actuary. Candidates should possess a strong background in calculus and an understanding of insurance and risk management principles to address the quantitative problems presented in the exam.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • General probability axioms
  • Combinatorics and set functions
  • Univariate random variable distributions
  • Expected value and moments
  • Multivariate discrete random variables
  • Linear combinations of variables

How to Prepare

Review the learning objectives provided in the CAS syllabus. Practice with the official sample questions to gain familiarity with the depth and format of the 30 questions. Ensure you are comfortable with the supporting calculus and the application of probability tools to insurance-related problems. Use the provided study guides to practice calculations for different random variable distributions and ensure you are prepared for the exam environment.

Domain Study Guidance

General Probability: Study Guidance

This domain covers probability concepts, including set functions, Venn diagrams, and sample spaces. Candidates calculate probabilities using addition, multiplication, and Bayes Theorem to solve problems related to general probability axioms and set theory applications for actuarial practice.

  • Set functions and Venn diagrams
  • Conditional probability and independence
  • Combinatorics and Bayes Theorem

Univariate Random Variables: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on discrete and continuous univariate random variables. Candidates apply these to insurance scenarios involving deductibles, coinsurance, and benefit limits, while mastering probability density functions, expected value, variance, and policy adjustment calculations required for actuarial practice.

  • Probability density functions
  • Expected value and variance
  • Policy adjustment calculations

Multivariate Random Variables: Study Guidance

This domain addresses multivariate discrete random variables, order statistics, and linear combinations of independent random variables. Candidates must demonstrate proficiency in joint probability functions, covariance, correlation, and linear combinations of variables to solve complex actuarial science problems.

  • Joint probability functions
  • Covariance and correlation
  • Linear combinations of variables

Exam-Day Guidance

The exam consists of 30 questions to be completed in 180 minutes. Manage your time effectively across the three domains. Choose the best answer from the provided options for each question, keeping in mind the required depth of understanding for all topics.
