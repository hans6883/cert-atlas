What This Exam Validates

The CAIA Level II exam is administered by the CAIA Association and covers seven domains of alternative investment knowledge. Candidates must pass Level I before registering. The exam uses a linear computer-based format combining multiple choice and essay questions. It assesses advanced analytical skills, valuation techniques, and risk management concepts across real assets, hedge funds, private equity, structured products, and other alternative strategies.

Who Should Take This Exam

Investment professionals working with alternatives who have completed Level I and want to deepen their expertise. Typical candidates include portfolio managers, allocators, and analysts handling real assets, hedge funds, or private equity.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Real assets valuation and analysis
  • Hedge fund strategy assessment
  • Private equity due diligence
  • Structured product mechanics
  • Risk management frameworks
  • Manager selection and regulation
  • Universal investment considerations

How to Prepare

Allocate study time across all seven domains, weighting effort to areas where you have less professional experience. Practice essay writing since the format includes constructed-response questions alongside multiple choice. Review the official CAIA curriculum and use the candidate handbook to confirm current exam specifications before scheduling.

Domain Study Guidance

Universal Investment Considerations: Study Guidance

This domain covers core investment concepts that apply across all alternative asset classes and establishes the analytical framework used throughout the rest of the Level II curriculum for alternative investments.

  • Cross-asset investment principles
  • Portfolio construction concepts
  • Performance measurement standards
  • Ethical and professional standards

Real Assets: Study Guidance

This domain addresses real assets including commodities, real estate, infrastructure, and natural resources. Candidates learn valuation methods, risk‑return characteristics, and portfolio integration considerations specific to tangible investments overall.

  • Commodity investment vehicles
  • Real estate equity and debt
  • Infrastructure project finance
  • Natural resources valuation

Hedge Funds: Study Guidance

This domain examines hedge fund strategies, structures, and fee arrangements. Candidates study how different hedge fund approaches generate returns, including liquidity constraints and performance measurement, and the associated risk profiles.

  • Long/short and event-driven strategies
  • Fund structure and fee models
  • Liquidity and redemption terms
  • Hedge fund return drivers

Private Equity: Study Guidance

This domain covers private equity including venture capital, buyouts, and growth equity. Candidates learn deal structuring, value creation methods, governance considerations, post‑investment monitoring, and exit pathways for portfolio.

  • Buyout and venture capital mechanics
  • Leveraged buyout modeling
  • Portfolio company value creation
  • Exit strategies and distributions

Structured Products: Study Guidance

This domain addresses structured products including their payoff structures, risk exposures, and use cases. Candidates learn how these instruments are constructed, priced, as well as regulatory considerations and market liquidity implications.

  • Structured product payoff structures
  • Credit derivatives and CDOs
  • Principal-protected products
  • Structured product risk profiles

Risk and Risk Management: Study Guidance

This domain covers risk measurement and risk management practices applied to alternative investments. Candidates study both quantitative risk models, including stress testing and scenario analysis, and qualitative oversight frameworks, such as governance processes.

  • Value at Risk and stress testing
  • Portfolio risk decomposition
  • Operational risk management
  • Counterparty and liquidity risk

Manager Selection, Due Diligence, and Regulation: Study Guidance

This domain addresses manager selection, operational due diligence, and the regulatory environment for alternative investments. Candidates learn how to evaluate and monitor external managers, including compliance testing, performance attribution, and ongoing monitoring protocols.

  • Manager due diligence processes
  • Operational risk assessment
  • Regulatory frameworks for alternatives
  • Fiduciary and compliance standards

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early with required identification and be prepared for a 265-minute linear computer-based exam. Pace yourself across multiple choice and essay sections, and read each question carefully before responding.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the CAIA Level II exam?

The CAIA Level II exam runs 265 minutes in a linear computer-based format. Candidates must complete all sections in sequence without the ability to return to earlier questions once a section is submitted.

What question types appear on the CAIA Level II exam?

The CAIA Level II exam includes both multiple choice questions and essay questions. The linear format means candidates progress through all questions sequentially within the 265-minute testing window.

How much does the CAIA Level II exam cost?

Level II registration costs approximately $995 according to current CAIA Association pricing. Candidates must pass Level I before they are eligible to register for Level II.

Do I need to pass Level I before taking Level II?

Yes, passing Level I is a prerequisite for Level II registration. The CAIA Association requires candidates to complete Level I successfully before they can sit for the Level II exam and meet all eligibility criteria.

Does the CAIA Level II certification require renewal?

Yes, the CAIA Level II certification requires renewal. Members must complete continuing education requirements to maintain their active status with the CAIA Association and must adhere to the association’s ethical standards.
