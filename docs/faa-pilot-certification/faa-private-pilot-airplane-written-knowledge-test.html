What This Exam Validates

The FAA Private Pilot Airplane Written Knowledge Test measures your understanding of aeronautical knowledge, risk management, and flight proficiency standards. Administered by FAA Pilot Certification, this exam covers 12 distinct domains ranging from preflight preparation to postflight procedures. It serves as a requirement for private pilot certification in the airplane category, including single-engine and multiengine classes, ensuring you possess the knowledge to act as pilot-in-command safely.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is intended for individuals seeking private pilot certification. Candidates should have completed their required flight training and ground school instruction to ensure they are prepared for the 60 questions presented during the test.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Preflight preparation and procedures
  • Airport and seaplane base operations
  • Takeoff and landing maneuvers
  • Navigation and flight planning
  • Emergency and night operations
  • Multiengine and instrument maneuvers

How to Prepare

Review the Airman Certification Standards to understand the knowledge and risk management requirements for each of the 12 domains. Use official FAA handbooks and study guides to practice the concepts covered in the 60 questions. Focus on understanding the practical application of weather data, navigation systems, and emergency procedures. Consistent practice with sample questions will help you meet the 70 percent passing score requirement for this certification.

Domain Study Guidance

Preflight Preparation: Study Guidance

This domain covers pilot qualifications, airworthiness requirements, and weather data analysis for flight planning. Candidates must understand how to interpret meteorological reports and ensure the aircraft is legally airworthy before beginning any flight operations.

  • Certification requirements
  • Weather products

Preflight Procedures: Study Guidance

Focuses on preflight assessment, flight deck management, and engine starting procedures. Candidates are expected to demonstrate knowledge of safety checks, cockpit organization, and the systematic process of starting the engine according to the aircraft flight manual.

  • Preflight assessment
  • Engine starting

Airport and Seaplane Base Operations: Study Guidance

Addresses communication, light signals, and traffic patterns at airports and seaplane bases. Understanding these procedures is required for maintaining safety while operating in the vicinity of an airport or when interacting with air traffic control services.

  • Traffic patterns
  • Light signals

Takeoffs, Landings, and Go-Arounds: Study Guidance

Covers various takeoff and landing techniques, including soft-field, short-field, and go-around procedures. Candidates must demonstrate an understanding of how to execute these maneuvers safely while accounting for wind conditions, runway length, and specific aircraft performance limitations.

  • Normal takeoffs
  • Short-field landings

Performance Maneuvers and Ground Reference Maneuvers: Study Guidance

Focuses on performance and ground reference maneuvers to maintain aircraft control. This domain requires knowledge of how to maintain a specific flight path relative to ground features while managing altitude, airspeed, and bank angles during various maneuvers.

  • Steep turns
  • Ground reference maneuvers

Navigation: Study Guidance

Covers pilotage, dead reckoning, and navigation system usage during flight. Candidates must be able to plan a cross-country flight, calculate fuel requirements, and utilize various navigation aids to maintain the intended course and reach the destination safely.

  • Pilotage
  • Navigation systems

Slow Flight and Stalls: Study Guidance

Addresses slow flight, stall recovery, and spin awareness. It is required for pilots to understand the aerodynamics of flight at low airspeeds and the proper recovery techniques to prevent or recover from stalls and inadvertent spins.

  • Slow flight
  • Stall recovery

Basic Instrument Maneuvers: Study Guidance

Focuses on basic instrument maneuvers and recovery from unusual flight attitudes. This domain ensures that pilots can maintain control of the aircraft solely by reference to flight instruments if they inadvertently encounter instrument meteorological conditions during flight.

  • Unusual attitude recovery
  • Instrument scan

Emergency Operations: Study Guidance

Covers emergency descent, systems malfunctions, and survival gear. Pilots must be prepared to handle unexpected situations, including engine failures, electrical fires, or other systems issues, by following established emergency checklists and maintaining aircraft control at all times.

  • Emergency descent
  • Systems malfunctions

Multiengine Operations: Study Guidance

Addresses multiengine operations, including one engine inoperative procedures. This domain is for pilots operating aircraft with more than one engine, focusing on the unique handling characteristics and safety procedures required during normal and emergency flight conditions.

  • One engine inoperative
  • Multiengine aerodynamics

Night Operations: Study Guidance

Focuses on the specific requirements and considerations for night flight operations. Pilots must understand the physiological effects of night flying, the importance of lighting systems, and the necessary precautions to ensure safety when operating in low-light environments.

  • Night operations
  • Lighting systems

Postflight Procedures: Study Guidance

Covers postflight procedures including parking and securing the aircraft. This domain emphasizes the importance of completing the flight by properly shutting down systems, securing the aircraft against weather, and documenting the flight in the appropriate logbooks.

  • Securing aircraft
  • Logbook entries

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early for your 150-minute exam. Ensure you have all required identification and documentation ready for verification. Manage your time effectively across the 60 questions to ensure you reach the 70 percent passing score required for certification.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The FAA Private Pilot Airplane Written Knowledge Test consists of 60 questions that cover the 12 domains of aeronautical knowledge, risk management, and flight proficiency standards required for safe operation of an aircraft.

What is the passing score?

To successfully pass the FAA Private Pilot Airplane Written Knowledge Test, you must achieve a minimum score of 70 percent on the exam, which demonstrates your competency in aeronautical knowledge and flight safety standards.

How long is the exam?

You are allotted 150 minutes to complete the 60 questions on the FAA Private Pilot Airplane Written Knowledge Test, allowing sufficient time to review each question and provide your answers for the certification.

Where can I find the current certification path?

For information regarding the long-term certification path or any updates to requirements, check with FAA Pilot Certification for the current certification path and any relevant regulatory updates for pilots seeking their license.
