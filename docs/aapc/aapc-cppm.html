What This Exam Validates

The Certified Physician Practice Manager (CPPM) exam is issued by AAPC. It tests operational and administrative knowledge across six domains: revenue cycle management, human resources, facilities and business services, information technology, compliance, and quality management. The credential is aimed at professionals who run the business side of physician practices and is delivered as a linear multiple-choice assessment.

Who Should Take This Exam

Practice administrators, office managers, billing supervisors, and operations staff who run day-to-day operations in a physician practice should consider the CPPM. Candidates typically have hands-on experience with scheduling, billing, staffing, and compliance workflows before sitting for the exam.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Revenue cycle management workflows
  • Human resources and staffing practices
  • Facilities and business operations
  • Information technology in medical practices
  • Healthcare compliance fundamentals
  • Quality management programs

How to Prepare

Start with the AAPC study materials and practice exams, then map each resource to the six exam domains. Build a study schedule that covers revenue cycle, HR, facilities, IT, compliance, and quality in rotation. Take timed practice tests to build pacing for the 240-minute window, and review any missed items against the official AAPC documentation.

Domain Study Guidance

Revenue Cycle Management: Study Guidance

Domain 1 covers revenue cycle management, the financial backbone of a physician practice. It addresses the processes that move a patient encounter from scheduling through final payment and reconciliation.

  • Patient registration and insurance verification
  • Coding, charge capture, and claim submission
  • Payment posting and accounts receivable follow-up
  • Denial management and appeals

Human Resources: Study Guidance

Domain 2 covers human resources management within a physician practice. It addresses the staffing, personnel, and workforce policies that keep a practice operating day after day, including hiring and retention.

  • Recruitment, hiring, and onboarding
  • Compensation, benefits, and performance evaluation
  • Employment law and workplace policies
  • Staff scheduling and retention

Facilities and Business Services: Study Guidance

Domain 3 covers facilities and business services for a physician practice. It addresses the physical plant, vendor relationships, and operational support functions that surround clinical care delivery each day.

  • Facility operations and maintenance
  • Vendor and supplier management
  • Business continuity and safety planning
  • Office services and supply chain

Information Technology: Study Guidance

Domain 4 covers information technology used in physician practice operations. It addresses the systems that store patient data, support billing, and enable day-to‑day daily communication across the office.

  • Electronic health records and practice management systems
  • Data security and access controls
  • Health information exchange and interoperability
  • IT support and system upgrades

Compliance: Study Guidance

Domain 5 covers compliance obligations that apply to physician practices. It addresses the federal laws, regulations, and internal programs that practices must follow to participate in healthcare programs.

  • Federal fraud and abuse laws including the Anti-Kickback Statute and Stark
  • HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules
  • Compliance program infrastructure and training
  • Coding and billing compliance audits

Quality Management: Study Guidance

Domain 6 covers quality management in a physician practice. It addresses the programs and measurement activities that practices use to track and improve patient care outcomes over time.

  • Quality measurement and reporting programs
  • Patient safety initiatives
  • Performance improvement methods
  • Accreditation and regulatory quality requirements

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early with valid identification and any required confirmation documents. Budget your time across the 100 questions inside the 240-minute window, and flag uncertain items to revisit before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The CPPM exam contains 100 multiple-choice questions delivered in a linear format. Candidates answer all items in a single sitting within the allotted testing window.

What is the passing score?

A passing score of 70 is required on the CPPM exam. Scores are reported against this benchmark, and candidates must meet or exceed it to earn the credential.

How long is the exam?

Candidates have 240 minutes to complete the CPPM exam. The full duration is allotted in a single sitting, so plan your pacing across all 100 questions.

How much does the exam cost?

The AAPC member price for the CPPM exam is $299, and the non-member price is $399. Membership discounts apply at the time of registration through AAPC.

How long is the certification valid?

The CPPM credential is valid for 2 years from the date of issuance. Renewal is required, so check with AAPC for the current continuing education and renewal requirements.

Is the CPPM exam still current?

The CPPM credential remains current and is administered by AAPC. It is valid for two years from issuance, after which renewal is required through continuing education and compliance activities as defined by AAPC.
