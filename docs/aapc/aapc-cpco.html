What This Exam Validates

The Certified Professional Compliance Officer (CPCO) exam is administered by AAPC. It tests knowledge across five domains: healthcare compliance programs, the regulatory environment, coding and billing compliance, risk management, and HIPAA and privacy. The credential is aimed at professionals who build and run compliance programs in healthcare settings, and it carries a passing score of 70.

Who Should Take This Exam

The CPCO suits compliance officers, practice managers, billing supervisors, auditors, and consultants who design or oversee healthcare compliance programs. Candidates typically have working knowledge of federal healthcare law, coding, and privacy rules before sitting for the exam.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Designing written compliance policies and codes of conduct
  • Applying federal anti-kickback, Stark, and False Claims Act principles
  • Auditing coding and billing for compliance risk
  • Running risk assessments, monitoring, and corrective action
  • Implementing HIPAA Privacy and Security safeguards
  • Responding to detected offenses and government reporting

How to Prepare

Start with AAPC's official CPCO study guide and practice exams, then map each chapter to the five exam domains. Read the OIG General Compliance Program Guidance for the compliance program and regulatory environment sections. Build a checklist of the seven compliance program elements and rehearse applying them to short scenarios before test day.

Domain Study Guidance

Healthcare Compliance Programs: Study Guidance

Domain 1 covers the structure of a healthcare compliance program. It addresses written policies, codes of conduct, training, communication channels, enforcement standards, auditing, and corrective action initiatives that form an effective program.

  • Written policies and code of conduct
  • Compliance officer, committee, and board roles
  • Training, communication, and enforcement
  • Auditing, monitoring, and corrective action

Regulatory Environment: Study Guidance

Domain 2 covers the federal regulatory environment that frames healthcare compliance. It includes the anti-kickback statute, physician self-referral law, False Claims Act, civil monetary penalty authorities, exclusion authorities, and criminal healthcare fraud statutes.

  • Federal anti-kickback statute and safe harbors
  • Physician self-referral (Stark) law
  • False Claims Act and civil monetary penalties
  • Exclusion authorities and criminal fraud statutes

Coding and Billing Compliance: Study Guidance

Domain 3 covers coding and billing compliance. It focuses on identifying improper billing patterns, applying correct coding rules, and using audits and monitoring to detect and prevent coding and billing errors.

  • Coding accuracy and documentation
  • Billing compliance and overpayment identification
  • Audit and monitoring processes
  • Corrective action for billing errors

Risk Management: Study Guidance

Domain 4 covers risk management within a compliance program. It addresses risk assessment, auditing and monitoring, and the response to detected offenses, including investigations, government reporting, and corrective action initiatives.

  • Risk assessment methodology
  • Auditing and monitoring activities
  • Investigations of detected violations
  • Reporting to government and corrective action

HIPAA and Privacy: Study Guidance

Domain 5 covers HIPAA and privacy. It addresses the HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules, breach response, patient rights, and the role of the compliance officer in protecting protected health information across the organization.

  • HIPAA Privacy Rule requirements
  • HIPAA Security Rule safeguards
  • Breach identification and response
  • Patient rights and minimum necessary use

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early with valid identification. The 180-minute, 90-question linear format allows about two minutes per question. Read each stem carefully, flag uncertain items, and review flagged questions before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the CPCO exam?

The CPCO exam contains 90 multiple-choice questions delivered in a linear format. Candidates answer all items in one sitting and cannot return to earlier questions after advancing through the exam.

What is the passing score for the CPCO exam?

The passing score for the CPCO exam is 70. Candidates must achieve at least 70 to earn the Certified Professional Compliance Officer credential from AAPC.

How long is the CPCO exam?

The CPCO exam lasts 180 minutes, giving candidates three hours to answer all 90 multiple‑choice questions presented in a linear, one‑pass format, meaning you cannot return to earlier items once you move forward.

How much does the CPCO exam cost?

The CPCO exam costs $299 for AAPC members and $399 for non-members. Membership pricing is available through AAPC for candidates who hold or pursue other AAPC credentials.

How long is the CPCO certification valid?

The CPCO certification is valid for 2 years. Renewal is required, and certificants must meet AAPC's continuing education and renewal requirements to maintain the credential.

What domains does the CPCO exam cover?

The CPCO exam covers five domains: Healthcare Compliance Programs, Regulatory Environment, Coding and Billing Compliance, Risk Management, and HIPAA and Privacy. Each domain tests a distinct area of compliance officer knowledge.
