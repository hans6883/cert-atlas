What This Exam Validates

The Certified Outpatient Coder (COC) exam, administered by AAPC, tests knowledge across nine domains: facility coding concepts, evaluation and management, outpatient surgery and procedures, radiology, pathology and laboratory, medicine and ancillary services, HCPCS Level II, ICD-10-CM, and revenue cycle management. It consists of 100 multiple-choice questions in a linear format, lasts 340 minutes, and requires a 70 % score to pass. The exam costs $399 for non-members and $299 for members, and the certification is valid for two years, with renewal required.

Who Should Take This Exam

Medical coders, billing specialists, health information technicians, and clinicians who regularly code outpatient services should pursue the COC credential to validate their expertise in facility and professional coding, E/M, and revenue cycle processes.

How to Prepare

Start with the official AAPC COC study guide, then complete practice exams on the AAPC website to gauge timing. Focus on the updated E/M guidelines and HCPCS Level II add-on codes. Review coding tables for radiology, pathology, and surgery. Schedule daily timed drills, track weak areas, and join an AAPC study group for discussion and clarification.

Domain Study Guidance

Facility Coding Concepts: Study Guidance

Facility Coding Concepts covers the structure of CPT, HCPCS, and ICD‑10‑CM codes used in outpatient settings, including modifiers, bundling rules, and payer-specific requirements. Mastery ensures accurate claim submission and reimbursement for facility services.

  • code structure
  • modifier usage
  • bundling rules

Evaluation and Management: Study Guidance

Evaluation and Management focuses on selecting the appropriate E/M level based on history, exam, and medical decision making, as well as time‑based reporting and the use of add‑on codes such as G2211.

  • E/M level selection
  • time‑based reporting
  • add‑on code G2211

Outpatient Surgery and Procedures: Study Guidance

Outpatient Surgery and Procedures addresses coding for surgical services performed in ambulatory settings, including CPT surgery sections, global periods, and appropriate use of modifiers for multiple procedures accurately.

  • surgical CPT sections
  • global period rules
  • procedure modifiers

Radiology: Study Guidance

Radiology examines imaging service codes, appropriate use of modifiers, and distinctions between diagnostic and interventional radiology procedures, including modality-specific guidelines and documentation requirements, to ensure correct billing and compliance.

  • imaging CPT codes
  • diagnostic vs interventional
  • radiology modifiers

Pathology and Laboratory: Study Guidance

Pathology and Laboratory covers coding for clinical lab tests, pathology services, and the application of CPT and HCPCS codes for specimen collection, analysis, and reporting, including molecular and genetic testing considerations.

  • lab test coding
  • pathology service codes
  • specimen collection

Medicine and Ancillary Services: Study Guidance

Medicine and Ancillary Services includes coding for office visits, preventive services, and ancillary procedures such as physical therapy, ensuring proper use of CPT and HCPCS Level II codes throughout the care continuum.

  • office visit coding
  • preventive service codes
  • ancillary procedure coding

HCPCS Level II: Study Guidance

HCPCS Level II focuses on non‑physician services, medical supplies, and equipment codes, including the correct application of add‑on and modifier codes for Medicare billing, as well as understanding regional carrier guidelines.

  • HCPCS Level II codes
  • add‑on codes
  • modifier application

ICD-10-CM: Study Guidance

ICD-10-CM addresses diagnosis coding conventions, chapter organization, and the selection of accurate codes to support the services reported in the other domains, including laterality and external cause coding considerations.

  • diagnosis code selection
  • chapter structure
  • coding conventions

Revenue Cycle Management: Study Guidance

Revenue Cycle Management reviews the end‑to‑end process from claim creation to payment, emphasizing documentation, compliance, denial management, and the role of coding accuracy in financial outcomes and revenue integrity.

  • claim creation
  • denial management
  • coding compliance

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early, bring a valid ID and your confirmation email, and ensure a quiet environment. Allocate roughly three minutes per question, flag uncertain items, and review flagged questions if time permits before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The COC exam contains 100 multiple‑choice questions presented in a linear format. All questions must be answered in the allotted 340‑minute window, and each correct answer contributes toward the 70 % passing threshold.

What is the passing score?

The exam requires a minimum score of 70 % to pass. With 100 questions, you need to answer at least 70 correctly. Scores are calculated automatically at the end of the testing session.

How long is the exam?

The testing session lasts 340 minutes, which is just over five and a half hours. This time includes all 100 questions and allows for review of flagged items before final submission.

Can I retake the COC exam if I fail?

If you do not achieve the passing score, AAPC permits a retake after a waiting period. You must pay the exam fee again and may use the same study resources to improve your performance.
