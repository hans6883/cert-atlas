What This Exam Validates

The Certified Professional – Food Safety credential is offered by NEHA to validate competencies required for industry professionals. This current examination evaluates candidate knowledge across seven distinct content domains, including the development of food safety policies, conducting inspection assessments, performing establishment plan reviews, and coordinating effective emergency responses during significant incidents.

Who Should Take This Exam

Food safety professionals, environmental health specialists, regulatory inspectors, and public health officials seeking to validate their knowledge of retail food protection, risk assessment, and operational safety standards.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Food safety policies and training
  • Inspection preparation and execution
  • Establishment plan review
  • Foodborne illness investigation
  • Recall activities management
  • Food defense practices
  • Emergency response coordination

How to Prepare

Review each of the seven content areas outlined in the official NEHA guide by studying hazard analysis principles, local regulations, and inspection equipment calibration methods to prepare carefully for all 140 questions and ensure complete readiness for every single objective tested on the examination.

Domain Study Guidance

Developing food safety policies, procedures, and training: Study Guidance

This domain covers the processes involved in developing food safety policies, standard operating procedures, and effective training programs designed to mitigate biological, chemical, and physical hazards in various operational environments.

  • Identify the five risk factors
  • Identify potential areas for contamination
  • Identify food pathogen attributes

Assessing food safety: Study Guidance

This domain focuses extensively on assessing overall food safety through equipment calibration, proper assembly of required inspection tools, and careful preparation for conducting facility inspections across diverse operational locations.

  • Prepare for an inspection
  • Assemble inspection equipment
  • Calibrate food safety equipment

Reviewing establishment plans: Study Guidance

This domain deals extensively with reviewing establishment blueprints and architectural plans, researching local laws and regulations, comparing facility menus against site conditions, and obtaining necessary operational licenses and permits.

  • Research local laws and regulations
  • Obtain licenses, permits, and variances (including special processes)
  • Compare menus to site conditions

Investigating foodborne illness: Study Guidance

This domain involves collecting initial public complaints, communicating effectively with appropriate regulatory agencies, and managing information exchange with both internal and external stakeholders during formal foodborne illness investigations.

  • Collect initial foodborne illness complaint
  • Communicate with appropriate agencies regarding the investigation process
  • Communicate with internal and external stakeholders regarding the investigation process

Performing recall activities: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers performing necessary recall activities such as training operational staff on safety procedures, conducting structured mock recalls, and submitting required regulatory documentation to ensure compliance.

  • Train staff on recall activities
  • Conduct mock recalls
  • Submit regulatory information

Managing food defense practices: Study Guidance

This particular domain centers on managing food defense practices, conducting vulnerability assessments, identifying potential security hazards, and developing a detailed protection plan to safeguard food supplies against intentional contamination.

  • Conduct food defense vulnerability assessments
  • Identify food defense hazards
  • Develop a food defense plan

Responding to emergencies: Study Guidance

This designated domain addresses responding to emergencies, coordinating multidisciplinary response teams, identifying associated operational risks, and determining parameters required for safely ceasing facility operations during significant incidents.

  • Determine parameters for ceasing operations
  • Coordinate the emergency response team
  • Identify risks associated with the emergency

Exam-Day Guidance

Manage your time carefully during the 150 minutes. Read each question carefully, noting that the 140 total items include both scored questions and unscored pilot questions.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The examination consists of a total of 140 questions, which includes 120 scored operational questions and 20 unscored pilot questions used for evaluation purposes by the administering organization.

What is the passing score?

The official passing score requirement remains unpublished on the official NEHA PDF guide, meaning candidates should check directly with the organization for specific scoring guidelines.

How long is the exam?

The official examination duration is exactly 2.5 hours, providing all registered candidates a total of 150 minutes to complete all questions included in the test format.

What certifying body administers the exam?

The examination is administered, managed, and certified by NEHA as a core component of its established professional credentialing program designed specifically for environmental health specialists.
