What This Exam Validates

The Medical College Admission Test (MCAT) is a computer-based exam that evaluates candidates across four distinct domains: chemical and physical foundations, critical analysis and reasoning, biological and biochemical foundations, and psychological, social, and biological foundations of behavior. Each of the four sections carries a weight of 25 percent, providing a balanced assessment of the knowledge and skills required for medical school. This evaluation ensures that all prospective students demonstrate the necessary proficiency in scientific inquiry and critical thinking before entering their medical education programs.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is intended for individuals applying to medical school. Candidates must be prepared for a 450-minute testing session that includes 230 questions. It is a fixed-form, computer-based assessment administered exclusively at Prometric test centers, requiring preparation to succeed in this academic environment.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • General chemistry and physics
  • Reading comprehension and inference
  • Biochemistry and organic chemistry
  • Psychology and sociology principles
  • Argument evaluation

How to Prepare

Review the official AAMC exam objectives to understand the scope of the four sections. Practice with passage-based and discrete questions to build familiarity with the fixed-form, computer-based format. Manage your time effectively during the 450-minute exam. Utilize the fee assistance program if eligible to reduce the standard registration cost of $335 to $135. Consistent practice and a deep understanding of the core scientific principles are required for achieving a competitive score on this assessment.

Domain Study Guidance

Chemical and Physical Foundations of Biological Systems (CPBS): general chemistry, physics, biochemistry, organic chemistry: Study Guidance

This section covers the chemical and physical principles that govern biological systems. It requires knowledge of general chemistry, physics, biochemistry, and organic chemistry to solve problems related to biological functions and systems.

  • General chemistry principles
  • Physics applications
  • Biochemistry and organic chemistry

Critical Analysis and Reasoning Skills (CARS): reading comprehension, inference, argument evaluation: Study Guidance

This domain tests your ability to comprehend, analyze, and evaluate complex passages. It focuses on reading comprehension, logical inference, and the evaluation of arguments presented in written text to determine your analytical capabilities.

  • Reading comprehension
  • Logical inference
  • Argument evaluation

Biological and Biochemical Foundations of Living Systems (BBFL): biology, biochemistry, organic chemistry, general chemistry: Study Guidance

This section examines biological and biochemical processes in living systems. It integrates concepts from biology, biochemistry, organic chemistry, and general chemistry to explain life at the molecular and cellular levels for medical students.

  • Biology and biochemistry
  • Organic and general chemistry

Psychological, Social, and Biological Foundations of Behavior (PSBF): psychology, sociology, biology: Study Guidance

This domain addresses the psychological, social, and biological foundations of behavior. It covers concepts from psychology and sociology as they relate to human biology and behavior to assess your understanding of complex social systems.

  • Psychology and sociology
  • Biological foundations of behavior

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive at your assigned Prometric test center for the computer-based exam. The test consists of four fixed-order sections with optional scheduled breaks. You may choose to void your scores at the end of the session if you feel your performance was not representative.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The exam consists of 230 questions distributed across four sections. Each section contributes equally to the total, with each domain representing 25 percent of the content to ensure a balanced and fair assessment of all candidates.

What is the passing score?

The AAMC does not define a specific passing score for the MCAT. Candidates should check with AAMC or their target medical schools for information regarding score requirements and the current certification path for their specific application goals.

How long is the exam?

The total duration of the exam is 450 minutes. This includes the time for the four sections, the tutorial, the void option, and the optional scheduled breaks between sections to allow for rest and mental preparation during the day.

What is the cost to register for the exam?

Standard registration is $335, while late registration is $375. Eligible applicants may use the fee assistance program to reduce the cost to $135. Rescheduling and cancellation fees also apply, so please review the official policy carefully before finalizing your registration.

Exam Details

Question TypesMultiple Choice (4 answer options), Passage-Based Questions, Discrete (stand-alone) Questions
FormatComputer-based, fixed-form (not adaptive); at Prometric test centers only — no at-home option. Four sections with optional scheduled breaks between each section. Total seated time approximately 7 hours 30 minutes (includes tutorial, void option, breaks). Section order is fixed: CPBS, CARS, BBFL, PSBF. Void option: candidate may void all scores at the end of the exam at no cost; voided scores are not reported.
Online ProctoringNot available
ID RequirementsOne government-issued, unexpired photo ID required (e.g., driver's license, passport). Name on ID must exactly match name on AAMC registration. A second acceptable form of ID is recommended as backup. Biometric data (fingerprint or palm vein scan) collected at some Prometric centers.
RenewalNot required -- MCAT scores are not certifications requiring renewal. Most medical schools accept MCAT scores up to 3 years old at the time of matriculation; some schools accept scores up to 2 years old, and some up to 5 years old. Verify score acceptance windows directly with each medical school. Candidates may retake the MCAT within lifetime attempt limits to obtain higher or more recent scores.
Retake PolicyUp to 3 MCAT attempts per testing year (Jan 1–Dec 31). Up to 4 attempts in any 2 consecutive testing years. Maximum 7 lifetime attempts. All non-voided scores are reported to schools (no score selection). Score release is approximately 30-35 days after the test date. Scores from voided exams or no-shows are not reported.
