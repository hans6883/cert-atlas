What This Exam Validates

The ABIM Internal Medicine Board Exam is the official certification assessment administered by the American Board of Internal Medicine. This examination evaluates the clinical knowledge, diagnostic reasoning, and professional competency of physicians across 18 distinct medical domains. Candidates must demonstrate proficiency in areas ranging from cardiovascular disease and endocrinology to infectious disease and oncology to achieve board certification in internal medicine and demonstrate their readiness for independent clinical practice.

Who Should Take This Exam

Physicians who have completed their residency training and are seeking formal board certification in internal medicine through the American Board of Internal Medicine should take this examination to validate their clinical expertise and professional standing.

How to Prepare

To prepare effectively, candidates should review the 18 specific domains tested on the exam. Focus your study time on high-weight domains such as cardiovascular disease, endocrinology, gastroenterology, infectious disease, pulmonary disease, and rheumatology. Utilize official resources to practice with board-style multiple-choice questions, which helps build the necessary test-taking proficiency required to navigate the 240 questions successfully during the examination session.

Domain Study Guidance

Allergy and Immunology: Study Guidance

This domain covers allergic and immunologic conditions that are evaluated during the internal medicine certification process, requiring a deep understanding of hypersensitivity reactions, immune system disorders, and various clinical allergy presentations.

  • Allergic conditions
  • Immunologic disorders
  • Hypersensitivity reactions

Cardiovascular Disease: Study Guidance

This section focuses on cardiovascular disease, representing a major portion of the examination content and requiring mastery of heart failure evaluation, ischemic heart disease, and various cardiac rhythm disturbances for patient care.

  • Heart failure evaluation
  • Ischemic heart disease
  • Cardiac rhythm management

Dermatology: Study Guidance

This domain examines dermatological disorders and the cutaneous manifestations of systemic diseases, testing the ability of the physician to identify skin lesions and diagnose underlying conditions through a standard physical examination process.

  • Skin disorders
  • Cutaneous lesions
  • Systemic disease manifestations

Endocrinology, Diabetes, and Metabolism: Study Guidance

This domain addresses metabolic disorders, diabetes management, and endocrine gland function, ensuring that candidates can effectively treat patients with hormonal imbalances and complex metabolic health issues in various clinical settings today.

  • Diabetes mellitus management
  • Endocrine disorders
  • Metabolic syndrome

Gastroenterology: Study Guidance

This section covers gastrointestinal tract disorders, liver diseases, and related pathologies, requiring candidates to demonstrate knowledge of diagnostic procedures and therapeutic interventions for common and complex digestive system conditions in adult patients.

  • Gastrointestinal diseases
  • Liver function evaluation
  • Digestive tract pathologies

Hematology: Study Guidance

This domain evaluates blood disorders, coagulation issues, and malignant hematology, testing the ability of the physician to interpret laboratory results and manage patients with various hematologic and oncologic conditions in clinical practice.

  • Hematologic disorders
  • Coagulation management
  • Malignant hematology

Infectious Disease: Study Guidance

This section focuses on infectious diseases, antibiotic stewardship, and pathogen identification, ensuring that physicians can accurately diagnose and treat a wide variety of bacterial, viral, and fungal infections effectively for their patients.

  • Community-acquired pneumonia
  • Antimicrobial selection
  • Infectious disease prevention

Miscellaneous: Study Guidance

This domain includes miscellaneous medical topics and cross-disciplinary clinical challenges that do not fit into other categories, testing the breadth of general internal medicine knowledge and clinical problem-solving skills for board certification.

  • Miscellaneous clinical presentations
  • General internal medicine topics
  • Cross-disciplinary challenges

Nephrology and Urology: Study Guidance

This domain covers renal function, urinary tract disorders, and electrolyte management, requiring candidates to understand the complexities of kidney disease and the maintenance of fluid and electrolyte balance in all adult patients.

  • Renal function assessment
  • Urological disorders
  • Electrolyte management

Neurology: Study Guidance

This section examines neurological conditions, central nervous system disorders, and diagnostic workups, ensuring that physicians can identify and manage common neurological issues encountered in an internal medicine clinical practice setting for patients.

  • Neurological evaluations
  • Central nervous system disorders
  • Diagnostic workups

Obstetrics and Gynecology: Study Guidance

This domain addresses obstetric and gynecological issues relevant to internal medicine practitioners, focusing on the management of women's health concerns and the intersection of pregnancy with various chronic medical conditions in clinical settings.

  • Women's health issues
  • Obstetric considerations in medicine
  • Gynecological health

Medical Oncology: Study Guidance

This section covers medical oncology, tumor types, and cancer treatment principles, testing the ability of the physician to provide supportive care and manage the complications associated with various cancer therapies in clinical practice.

  • Medical oncology management
  • Cancer therapies
  • Supportive care

Ophthalmology: Study Guidance

This domain evaluates ophthalmological conditions and eye manifestations of systemic disease, ensuring that physicians can recognize ocular symptoms that may indicate underlying systemic health issues during a routine patient examination in clinical practice.

  • Eye disorders
  • Ocular manifestations
  • Systemic disease indicators

Otolaryngology and Dental Medicine: Study Guidance

This section addresses otolaryngology and dental medicine topics within internal medicine, focusing on upper respiratory tract conditions and oral health issues that impact the overall well-being of the adult patient in clinical settings.

  • Upper respiratory tract conditions
  • Dental and oral manifestations
  • Otolaryngology basics

Psychiatry: Study Guidance

This domain focuses on psychiatric disorders, behavioral health, and mental status evaluations, ensuring that physicians can identify common mental health conditions and coordinate appropriate care for their patients in a clinical practice setting.

  • Psychiatric conditions
  • Behavioral assessments
  • Mental status evaluations

Pulmonary Disease: Study Guidance

This section covers pulmonary diseases, respiratory mechanics, and lung pathologies, requiring candidates to demonstrate expertise in managing conditions such as pneumonia, asthma, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in a clinical practice setting.

  • Pneumonia management
  • Respiratory failure evaluation
  • Lung pathologies

Rheumatology and Orthopedics: Study Guidance

This domain examines rheumatologic diseases, musculoskeletal disorders, and orthopedic conditions, ensuring that physicians can diagnose and manage joint pain, autoimmune conditions, and other musculoskeletal issues commonly seen in adult patients during clinical practice.

  • Rheumatologic disorders
  • Musculoskeletal assessments
  • Autoimmune conditions

Geriatric Syndromes: Study Guidance

This section focuses on geriatric syndromes and health issues affecting older adult populations, requiring physicians to understand the unique physiological and social needs of elderly patients in a clinical setting for better outcomes.

  • Geriatric conditions

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The ABIM Internal Medicine Board Exam consists of 240 questions that are distributed across 18 distinct medical domains, designed to test your clinical knowledge and decision-making abilities for board certification.

What is the passing score?

You should check with the American Board of Internal Medicine for specific details regarding passing score thresholds, as the methodology for determining success is established by their official board standards for all candidates.

How long is the exam?

The total duration of the examination is 600 minutes, which provides sufficient time for candidates to complete all 240 questions while maintaining focus and accuracy throughout the entire testing session for the certification.

Who administers the ABIM Internal Medicine Board Exam?

The American Board of Internal Medicine is the organization that administers this exam and certifies candidates who meet the required professional standards and demonstrate clinical competency in internal medicine for their medical practice.
