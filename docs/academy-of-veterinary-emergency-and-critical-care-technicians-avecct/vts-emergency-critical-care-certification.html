What This Exam Validates

The VTS Emergency and Critical Care Certification is administered by the Academy of Veterinary Emergency and Critical Care Technicians (AVECCT). The assessment covers 13 distinct clinical domains, including anesthesia, cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal, urinary, fluid balance, endocrine, hemolymphatic, neurologic, musculoskeletal, toxicology, reproductive, and triage care, across exactly 200 total questions for candidates.

Who Should Take This Exam

Veterinary technicians pursuing advanced credentials in emergency and critical care medicine. Candidates must meet experience prerequisites set by AVECCT before applying for the certification path.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Anesthesia and analgesia management
  • Cardiovascular stabilization
  • Respiratory support techniques
  • Fluid and electrolyte therapy
  • Emergency triage and care

How to Prepare

Review the 13 exam domains very carefully, focusing on patient stabilization and monitoring protocols across all clinical sections during your studies. Study official Academy of Veterinary Emergency and Critical Care Technicians guidelines and utilize pre-application instructions to verify eligibility requirements before taking the 200-question exam.

Domain Study Guidance

Anesthesia/Analgesia: Study Guidance

This specific domain focuses on the delivery, monitoring, and management of anesthesia and analgesia protocols during various emergency procedures and stabilizing critical veterinary patients effectively in hospital settings.

  • Anesthetic monitoring
  • Pain management protocols

Cardiovascular: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers cardiovascular assessments, heart rate evaluations, pulse quality analysis, and the clinical management of circulatory shock or cardiac arrhythmias in animals requiring urgent care and medical support.

  • Capillary refill time
  • Blood pressure monitoring

Respiratory: Study Guidance

This specific domain addresses respiratory rate and effort evaluations, oxygenation status, airway maintenance procedures, and the overall management of pleural space diseases in critical veterinary patients needing ventilation.

  • Airway clearance
  • Ventilation support

Gastrointestinal (includes hepatic, acute abdomen, pancreatitis,and peritonitis): Study Guidance

This specific domain encompasses gastrointestinal emergencies, including hepatic issues, acute abdomen cases, pancreatitis, peritonitis, and associated clinical complications requiring immediate medical intervention and patient stabilization procedures within clinical practice environments.

  • Abdominal assessment
  • Gastrointestinal disorders

Urinary: Study Guidance

This specific domain examines urinary system emergencies, including urethral obstructions, acute kidney injury, and various urinary tract disorders requiring immediate veterinary medical attention and ongoing patient care support.

  • Urinary function evaluation
  • Obstruction management

Fluid/Electrolyte/Acid base: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers fluid therapy administration, electrolyte imbalances, and complex acid-base disturbances routinely encountered in critically ill veterinary patients requiring intensive medical management and constant monitoring during hospital stays.

  • Fluid resuscitation
  • Acid-base balance

Endocrine/Metabolic: Study Guidance

This specific domain addresses endocrine and metabolic emergencies such as diabetic crises, severe hypoglycemia, and various other hormonal or metabolic derangements affecting critically ill veterinary patients during hospital stays.

  • Metabolic derangements
  • Endocrine disorders

Hemolymphatic/Immunologic (including Oncology): Study Guidance

This specific domain focuses on hemolymphatic, immunologic, and oncological emergencies, including severe anemia, bleeding disorders, and complex immune-mediated conditions requiring blood product support and nursing care during hospital treatments.

  • Blood product transfusion
  • Coagulation disorders

Neurologic: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers neurologic assessments, mental state evaluations, trauma to the central or peripheral nervous systems, and effective seizure management strategies for animal patients undergoing emergency evaluation.

  • Level of consciousness
  • Neurologic injury evaluation

Musculoskeletal: Study Guidance

This specific domain examines musculoskeletal injuries, fractures, and related orthopedic conditions safely managed within emergency and critical care hospital settings by trained veterinary technicians handling complex trauma cases daily.

  • Trauma assessment
  • Musculoskeletal stabilization

Toxicology/Pharmacology: Study Guidance

This specific domain addresses toxicology cases, environmental poison exposures, and pharmacological management protocols designed specifically for poisoned or intoxicated animals requiring immediate emergency care and constant observation by clinical staff.

  • Toxin ingestion management
  • Pharmacological interventions

Reproductive: Study Guidance

This specific domain covers reproductive emergencies such as dystocia, eclampsia, and other breeding-related acute conditions occurring in small and large animals that require urgent veterinary medical intervention and surgery.

  • Dystocia management
  • Reproductive care

Misc., Triage/Emergency Care (May include: burns, ophthalmology, envenomation, hyper/hypothermia, exotics, procedures/equipment not specifically related to other systems, etc.): Study Guidance

This specific domain encompasses miscellaneous topics including primary triage, burns, ophthalmology, envenomation, thermal regulation, and emergency procedures not specifically categorized under other distinct veterinary systems during clinical practice evaluations.

  • Primary survey triage
  • Environmental emergency care

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive fully prepared for the challenging 200-question examination. Manage your time carefully across all 13 clinical domains and utilize proper triage and stabilization principles during the assessment.
