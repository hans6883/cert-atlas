What This Exam Validates

ACCUPLACER Arithmetic (exam code AR) is a computer-based placement test issued by ACCUPLACER. It measures pre-algebra skills across five domains: whole number operations, fraction operations, decimal operations, percent, and number comparisons and equivalents. The test is adaptive and uses multiple-choice items. The test adapts to the examinee's ability level, presenting increasingly challenging items as correct answers are given, and it is used by colleges nationwide to place students into appropriate math courses.

Who Should Take This Exam

Students entering college who need to confirm readiness for entry-level math coursework. It suits incoming freshmen, dual-enrollment candidates, and anyone whose institution uses ACCUPLACER for course placement.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Whole number operations
  • Fraction operations
  • Decimal operations
  • Percent calculations
  • Number comparisons and equivalents

How to Prepare

Work through each of the five domains using a pre-algebra textbook, focusing on the four arithmetic operations with whole numbers, fractions, and decimals. Practice converting between fractions, decimals, and percents, and comparing values across forms. Take timed sets of 20 multiple-choice items to build pacing for the 45-minute session.

Domain Study Guidance

Whole number operations: Study Guidance

Whole number operations covers addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with non-negative integers, including place value, rounding, and the standard algorithms for carrying and borrowing. Students must also interpret word problems, estimate results, and apply these operations in real-world contexts.

  • Place value and rounding
  • Addition and subtraction algorithms
  • Multiplication with partial products
  • Long division

Fraction operations: Study Guidance

Fraction operations covers working with proper and improper fractions, equivalent fractions, common denominators, and the four arithmetic operations applied to fractional quantities. Learners also simplify complex fractions, solve mixed-number problems, and translate fractional information into visual representations.

  • Equivalent fractions and simplification
  • Common denominators
  • Multiplying and dividing fractions
  • Adding and subtracting fractions

Decimal operations: Study Guidance

Decimal operations covers place value in decimal notation and the four arithmetic operations, including rounding decimals and converting between decimal and fractional forms. Students practice aligning decimal points for addition and subtraction, estimate products, and understand how decimal placement affects value.

  • Decimal place value
  • Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of decimals
  • Rounding decimals
  • Decimal-to-fraction conversion

Percent: Study Guidance

Percent covers the meaning of percent, conversion between percent, decimal, and fraction forms, and percent calculations including percent of a number and percent change. The domain also includes solving real-life scenarios such as discounts, tax calculations, and interest, reinforcing the practical use of percent.

  • Percent-decimal-fraction conversions
  • Percent of a number
  • Percent increase and decrease
  • Applications to real-world contexts

Number comparisons and equivalents: Study Guidance

Number comparisons and equivalents covers ordering whole numbers, fractions, and decimals, recognizing equivalent representations, and using comparison symbols and number lines. Students learn to compare values using number lines, convert between forms to verify equivalence, and justify ordering decisions with clear reasoning.

  • Ordering whole numbers, fractions, and decimals
  • Recognizing equivalent forms
  • Using greater-than and less-than symbols
  • Placing values on a number line

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive early with required identification. The 45-minute, 20-question adaptive format means pacing matters; skip and return if your test platform allows it. Read each multiple-choice item carefully before selecting.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the exam?

The ACCUPLACER Arithmetic test contains 20 questions. It is delivered as a computer-based adaptive multiple-choice exam, so the difficulty of later items adjusts based on earlier responses.

What is the passing score?

ACCUPLACER Arithmetic is a placement test, not a pass/fail certification. Each institution sets its own score thresholds for course placement, so check with your college for the cutoff it uses.

How long is the exam?

The exam runs for 45 minutes. That time limit covers all 20 adaptive multiple-choice items, so practicing timed sets helps with pacing on test day.

How much does the exam cost?

Pricing is set by the institution administering the test. ACCUPLACER Arithmetic is typically free or $10-25, depending on the college, so confirm the fee with your testing site before registering.

What topics are covered on the exam?

The exam assesses five content areas: whole‑number operations, fraction operations, decimal operations, percent calculations, and number‑comparison/equivalence skills. Together these domains evaluate the pre‑algebra arithmetic abilities that colleges rely on for appropriate course placement decisions.

Is the exam current?

Yes, ACCUPLACER Arithmetic (code AR) remains a current placement exam used by many colleges. While it is not a certification, it continues to serve as a standard measure of pre‑algebra readiness. For any updates or alternative pathways, contact ACCUPLACER directly.
