What This Exam Validates

The LEED AP Interior Design and Construction (ID+C) exam evaluates professional knowledge regarding the sustainable design, construction, and improvement of commercial interior spaces. Administered by USGBC, this assessment spans 8 domains. Candidates must answer 100 questions within a 120 minutes time limit and achieve a passing score of 170/200 to earn the credential.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is intended for professionals involved in the design, construction, and improvement of commercial interior spaces. Candidates should have experience with LEED projects and a practical understanding of sustainable building systems, water efficiency, and material selection within the interior design and construction industry.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • LEED process management
  • Integrative design and planning
  • Location and transportation analysis
  • Sustainable site development
  • Water efficiency strategies
  • Energy and atmosphere performance
  • Material and resource optimization
  • Indoor environmental quality standards

How to Prepare

Review the official USGBC candidate handbook to understand the exam structure across all 8 domains. Focus on the core requirements for water reduction, energy performance, and indoor air quality. Allocate study time to practice all 100 questions during prep sessions, ensuring you can manage the 120 minutes duration and reach the required passing score of 170/200 on test day.

Domain Study Guidance

LEED Process and Project Priorities: Study Guidance

This domain covers the management of the LEED process and the prioritization of project goals to ensure sustainability. Candidates must understand how to align project objectives with green building standards throughout the design and construction phases of commercial interior projects.

  • LEED process management
  • Project priority setting

Integrative Process Planning and Assessments: Study Guidance

This section focuses on the early analysis of building systems to achieve synergies across various disciplines. Candidates learn how to integrate energy and water system analysis to optimize project performance and ensure that all design decisions support long-term sustainability goals effectively.

  • Integrative process planning
  • Energy and water system analysis

Location and Transportation: Study Guidance

This domain covers site selection and transportation choices designed to reduce environmental impact. Candidates must evaluate neighborhood development locations, access to quality transit, and the implementation of bicycle facilities to support sustainable commuting and reduce the carbon footprint of the project.

  • Neighborhood development location
  • Access to quality transit
  • Bicycle facilities

Sustainable Sites: Study Guidance

This area addresses land conservation and the protection of existing infrastructure through smart site development. Candidates study the importance of surrounding density and diverse use proximity to minimize the impact on natural land and maximize the utility of existing urban environments.

  • Surrounding density
  • Diverse use proximity

Water Efficiency: Study Guidance

This domain covers methods to reduce indoor water consumption through the use of efficient fixtures and appliances. Candidates learn about indoor water use reduction strategies and the specific WaterSense labeling requirements necessary to meet the established standards for sustainable interior water management.

  • Indoor water use reduction
  • WaterSense labeling requirements

Energy and Atmosphere: Study Guidance

This section focuses on energy performance, commissioning, and the use of renewable energy. Candidates must understand fundamental commissioning, minimum energy performance requirements, and refrigerant management to ensure that interior spaces operate efficiently and minimize their overall impact on the atmosphere.

  • Fundamental commissioning
  • Minimum energy performance
  • Refrigerant management

Materials and Resources: Study Guidance

This domain addresses waste management and the selection of sustainable materials. Candidates study the requirements for recyclable storage, waste management planning, and life-cycle impact reduction to ensure that materials used in interior construction are environmentally responsible and support circular economy principles.

  • Recyclable storage
  • Waste management planning
  • Life-cycle impact reduction

Indoor Environmental Quality: Study Guidance

This section covers strategies to improve indoor air quality and occupant comfort. Candidates learn about indoor air quality performance, the selection of low-emitting materials, and the importance of thermal comfort and lighting to create healthy and productive interior environments for all building occupants.

  • Indoor air quality performance
  • Low-emitting materials
  • Thermal comfort and lighting

Exam-Day Guidance

Arrive prepared to complete all 100 questions within the 120 minutes total exam duration. Carefully manage your pace across all 8 domains to achieve the required passing score of 170/200.
