What This Exam Validates

The U.S. Coast Guard (National Maritime Center) administers the USCG Able Seaman Credential Examination to ensure mariners possess the essential skills for safe vessel operations. The examination consists of 100 questions divided into 2 distinct modules. Candidates must achieve a 70% passing score per module to qualify for professional maritime service on U.S. waters.

Who Should Take This Exam

This exam is intended for mariners pursuing an Able Seaman credential. Candidates must demonstrate practical knowledge of navigation rules and deck safety procedures to meet the official U.S. Coast Guard requirements for professional maritime service and vessel operations.

Skills You Should Be Ready to Demonstrate

  • Navigation and Rules of the Road
  • Deck General Safety
  • Collision avoidance procedures
  • Vessel safety protocols
  • Sound and light signals
  • Steering and sailing rules

How to Prepare

Review the official U.S. Coast Guard (National Maritime Center) exam guides for navigation and deck safety. Study the International and Inland Navigation Rules, including light and shape requirements, sound signals, and steering rules across the 2 modules. Familiarize yourself with the conversion tables and legal citations provided in the official documentation to ensure you meet the 70% passing score requirement for each module. Consistent practice with these materials is important for success.

Domain Study Guidance

Q450-Navigation and Rules of the Road: Study Guidance

This domain covers the International and Inland Navigation Rules, including steering, sailing, sound signals, and lights. It focuses on the legal requirements for vessel operations and collision avoidance during maritime navigation to ensure safety at sea.

Q451-Deck General Safety: Study Guidance

This domain addresses general safety practices required for safe deck operations across vessels. It covers the responsibilities of the master and crew, documented safety precautions, and standardized operational safety protocols across various maritime environments and vessel types.
